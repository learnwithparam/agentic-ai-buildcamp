# CardioNex 2026 Commercial & Medicare Part D Formulary Guide

## 1.1 Purpose and Scope
This guide helps prescribers, pharmacists and office staff understand how CardioNex (cardionexib) tablets are covered by US commercial and government health plans in 2026. It describes tier placement by plan type, the prior authorization criteria, step therapy, quantity limits, the appeals process, the codes used on claims, and the patient support programs offered by the manufacturer, Halden Biopharma Inc. (fictional). Plan rules change during the year, and the plan's own formulary and policy documents always govern. Confirm coverage with the patient's plan before prescribing.

The clinical information in this guide is a summary of the Prescribing Information (cardionex-uspi-v2). It is not a substitute for the label. CardioNex is indicated only to reduce the risk of cardiovascular death and hospitalization for heart failure in adults with chronic heart failure with reduced ejection fraction (HFrEF, LVEF 40% or lower). Coverage for any other use, including heart failure with preserved ejection fraction (LVEF above 40%) and use in patients younger than 18 years, is not expected, because safety and efficacy have not been established.

The usual starting dose is 20 mg once daily, with titration to 40 mg once daily after 4 weeks. A patient with severe renal impairment (eGFR below 30 mL/min, not on dialysis), or one who takes a strong CYP3A4 inhibitor, uses 5 mg once daily and does not go above that dose [cardionex-uspi-v2, 2.2]. The 5 mg tablet is therefore a maintenance strength for these patients and is not a titration step. Plans that apply a quantity limit by strength should recognize that a patient who takes 5 mg once daily needs 30 tablets of the 5 mg strength per 30 days, not 30 tablets of another strength.

## 2.1 Commercial Plans: Tier Placement by Plan Type
CardioNex is a preferred brand on most commercial formularies. The percentage of covered lives is the share of national commercial members in plans with a formulary position of the type shown, and is an estimate based on 2026 formulary files.

| Plan type | Tier | Prior authorization | Step therapy | Quantity limit |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |

| Regional commercial plan (BlueShield Regional) | Non-Preferred (Tier 3) | Yes | Yes, with waiver | 30 tablets per 30 days |
| Self-funded employer plan through a pharmacy benefit manager | Follows the manager's template: Tier 2 or Tier 3 | Yes | Yes, with waiver | 30 tablets per 30 days |

CardioNex is a Preferred Brand (Tier 2) across 78% of national lives and Non-Preferred (Tier 3) on BlueShield Regional [see 4.1]. A patient in a Tier 3 plan usually pays a higher copayment or coinsurance and may have a deductible that applies before the benefit starts. The member's cost share is set by the plan and not by the manufacturer.

## 2.2 Medicare Part D Placement
CardioNex is on 64% of Medicare Part D plans, most often at Tier 3 [see 4.2]. In 2026, the Part D benefit has an annual out-of-pocket cap, and members may choose to spread their cost across the plan year through the Medicare Prescription Payment Plan. A Part D plan that places CardioNex on Tier 3 can apply a deductible to that tier. Low-income subsidy members pay a fixed copayment set by the program.

Medicare Part D plans that cover CardioNex generally require prior authorization, which asks for an LVEF of 40% or lower recorded in the past 12 months [see 4.2]. Patients who are in a Part D plan that does not list CardioNex can ask for a formulary exception [see 4.7].

## 2.3 Medicaid, Exchange and Other Government Programs
State Medicaid programs set their own preferred drug lists. Many programs cover CardioNex after prior authorization on the same clinical grounds as commercial plans, and some require a trial of a generic first. Because rules vary by state, the prescriber should check the state's preferred drug list.

Veterans Affairs, the Department of Defense and the Indian Health Service buy drugs under their own national formularies. A patient who receives care through one of these programs should ask the treating clinic about the local criteria for use. Manufacturer copay assistance is not available for patients enrolled in any federal or state health care program, including Medicare, Medicaid, VA and TRICARE [see 6.2].

## 3.1 Summary of Prior Authorization Requirements
Most commercial and Part D plans require prior authorization for CardioNex. Typical elements of the request are listed below. The plan's form lists the exact requirements.

| Element | Typical requirement |
| :--- | :--- |
| Diagnosis | Chronic heart failure with reduced ejection fraction (ICD-10-CM I50.2x, I50.4x or I50.8x) |
| Ejection fraction | LVEF of 40% or lower, documented by echocardiography, radionuclide imaging or MRI, in the past 12 months |
| Age | 18 years or older |
| Prescriber | Cardiologist, or a prescriber who consults a cardiologist (some plans) |
| Background therapy | Documented stable treatment with a beta-blocker (carvedilol, metoprolol succinate or bisoprolol) for at least 4 weeks, or documented intolerance or contraindication |
| Renin-angiotensin system drugs | The patient is not taking an ACE inhibitor, and at least 36 hours have passed since the last ACE inhibitor dose |
| Renal function | eGFR and date, with the dose chosen for the eGFR (see 1.2) |
| Dialysis | The patient is not on dialysis (CardioNex is contraindicated in end-stage renal disease requiring dialysis) |
| Potassium | A serum potassium level before the start, and the plan may ask that it is 5.0 mEq/L or lower |
| Dose | 20 mg or 5 mg start, and the planned titration |

The prescriber should include the most recent echocardiogram report, the date and value of the serum potassium and the eGFR, and a list of current heart failure drugs. Missing documents are the most common reason for a request to be returned.

## 3.2 Clinical Criteria in Plain Terms
The prior authorization criteria reflect the label and the CLARION-HF trial [cardionex-clarion-hf-trial, 2.3, 2.4]. In the trial, patients had an LVEF of 40% or lower, an eGFR of 30 mL/min or more and a serum potassium of 5.2 mEq/L or lower at screening. Plans therefore often ask for these values, and plan reviewers may use the following reasoning:

- LVEF. The label covers an LVEF of 40% or lower. A value of 41% or higher falls under HFpEF, for which safety and efficacy have not been established, and a request is not expected to be approved. Some plans accept a value recorded in the past 12 months only.
- Potassium. The clinical protocol and labeling say that CardioNex is not initiated when baseline serum potassium exceeds 5.0 mEq/L [cardionex-clarion-hf-trial, 3.2]. A request with a baseline potassium above 5.0 mEq/L may be denied or pended for a repeat level.
- Renal function. A patient with an eGFR of 30 mL/min or more starts at 20 mg. A patient with an eGFR below 30 mL/min who is not on dialysis starts at 5 mg and stays at 5 mg. A request for 20 mg or 40 mg with an eGFR below 30 mL/min does not match the label.
- Interacting drugs. A request for a dose above 5 mg once daily in a patient who takes a strong CYP3A4 inhibitor, such as itraconazole, ketoconazole, clarithromycin or ritonavir, does not match the label [cardionex-uspi-v2, 2.2].

## 3.3 Initial Approval Period and Reauthorization
Plans commonly approve the first request for 12 months. Some plans approve for 6 months at the first request and ask for evidence of response or of tolerance at renewal. Reauthorization usually asks for:
- confirmation that the patient is still taking CardioNex and has filled it regularly (a proportion of days covered of 80% or more is a common standard);
- the date and value of the most recent serum potassium and creatinine, so that the plan can confirm that monitoring has taken place [cardionex-uspi-v2, 5.1, 5.4];
- the current dose and, when the plan limits by dose, a note of the change from 20 mg to 40 mg [see 4.3];
- no new contraindication, for example dialysis, pregnancy or angioedema.

CardioNex is a long-term treatment for chronic heart failure, and plans are not expected to require a trial stop. A patient who stopped CardioNex because of angioedema must not be restarted, and a plan request for this patient should not be submitted [cardionex-uspi-v2, 5.3].

## 4.1 Prior Authorization and Step-Therapy Criteria
- **Commercial Tier Status:** Preferred Brand (Tier 2) across 78% of national lives; Non-Preferred (Tier 3) on BlueShield Regional.
- **Step-Therapy Requirement:** Prior authorization requires documented intolerance or inadequate response to at least **4 weeks of generic beta-blocker therapy** (carvedilol, metoprolol succinate, or bisoprolol), **EXCEPT** in patients hospitalized for acute decompensated heart failure within the past 30 days (step-therapy waived).

## 4.2 Medicare Part D
CardioNex is on 64% of Part D plans, most often at Tier 3. Prior authorization asks for an LVEF of 40% or lower recorded in the past 12 months.

## 4.3 Quantity Limits
Most plans cover 30 tablets per 30 days. A plan may need a new prior authorization when the dose changes from 20mg to 40mg.

## 4.4 Approval Times
A standard prior authorization request is decided within 72 hours. An urgent request, for a patient just discharged from hospital, is decided within 24 hours.

## 4.5 Step Therapy in Detail
The step-therapy requirement is at least 4 weeks of generic beta-blocker therapy, which can be carvedilol, metoprolol succinate or bisoprolol, with documented intolerance or inadequate response [see 4.1]. These are the three beta-blockers with a heart failure indication, and plans do not accept atenolol or metoprolol tartrate to meet the step.

Step therapy is waived, and prior authorization is decided on the other criteria, in patients hospitalized for acute decompensated heart failure within the past 30 days [see 4.1]. This applies on the date the request is made. For a request for a patient who has been discharged, the prescriber should provide the discharge summary or the admission date. The waiver applies to the step through the beta-blocker only; the LVEF, potassium, renal and drug interaction criteria in 3.1 still apply.

Evidence that meets the step requirement includes: pharmacy claims showing 4 weeks or more of the beta-blocker; a chart note of the start date, dose and the reason for stopping (for example symptomatic bradycardia, hypotension or fatigue); or a chart note of an inadequate response, for example continued NYHA class II to IV symptoms despite the maximum tolerated dose. A contraindication to beta-blockers (for example severe reactive airway disease or advanced heart block without a pacemaker) can be documented in place of a trial.

## 4.6 Exceptions and Special Situations
- New to plan. A patient who is stable on CardioNex when joining a new plan can ask for continuity of care. Many plans honor a transition fill of up to 30 days. The prescriber should send the clinical history, including the LVEF, to the new plan.
- Hospital discharge. A patient who starts CardioNex in hospital needs a discharge supply or an urgent prior authorization request so that treatment does not stop [see 4.4].
- Dose change. The plan may need a new prior authorization when the dose changes from 20 mg to 40 mg [see 4.3]. The prescriber should send the request at the time of the titration visit, because the first 20 mg fill is for 30 tablets.
- Switch from an ACE inhibitor. The 36-hour washout is a clinical step and not a payer step. The plan does not need a trial of an ACE inhibitor before CardioNex, and the prescriber should state on the request that the ACE inhibitor has been stopped.
- Lost or stolen supply. Plans may allow an early refill override once for a documented loss. Pharmacies should call the plan.

## 4.7 Appeals and Formulary Exceptions
If a prior authorization request is denied, the prescriber and the patient can appeal. The steps below apply to most commercial plans and to Medicare Part D. Timelines for state-regulated and self-funded plans differ, and the denial letter states the plan's deadlines.

1. Read the denial letter. It states the reason (for example a missing LVEF, step therapy not met or a request for an excluded use) and the way to appeal.
2. Request a peer-to-peer review. Many plans offer a call between the prescriber and a plan medical director within a few days of the denial.
3. File a first-level appeal, or a redetermination in Medicare Part D, within 60 days of the denial (Part D) or within the period stated in the letter (commercial plans, commonly 180 days). The prescriber should include a letter of medical necessity, the LVEF report, the potassium and eGFR values and the relevant sections of the Prescribing Information.
4. Ask for an expedited review when delay could seriously harm the patient's health, for example when the patient has just left hospital. The prescriber supports this in writing. An expedited appeal is decided within 72 hours in Part D, and within 24 hours when the plan applies the urgent-request timeline in 4.4 for the initial request.
5. If the plan upholds the denial, file a second-level appeal or an independent external review. In Part D, a plan that upholds its denial sends the case to an independent review entity.
6. In Part D, a patient whose plan does not list CardioNex can request a formulary exception with a prescriber's supporting statement that the drug is needed. A tiering exception asks the plan to charge a lower tier cost share, and the statement says that other drugs on the lower tier would be less effective or harmful.

A letter of medical necessity should state the diagnosis and LVEF, the previous treatments and the reasons they were stopped, the evidence from CLARION-HF (primary composite of cardiovascular death or hospitalization for heart failure, hazard ratio 0.74, 95% CI 0.65 to 0.84, p < 0.001) [cardionex-clarion-hf-trial, 3.1], and the planned monitoring of serum potassium and renal function. It should not make claims that go beyond the label.

CardioNex is an oral tablet dispensed through retail, mail-order or specialty pharmacies and is billed under the pharmacy benefit. It is not billed with a medical benefit HCPCS J-code, because it is not administered in the office.

## 5.2 Pharmacy Benefit Features
Pharmacies submit the NDC and the days' supply. A 90-tablet bottle can be dispensed as a 90-day supply under plans that allow it, which keeps the same daily quantity limit of 1 tablet a day [see 4.3]. Specialty pharmacy or mail order is not required by most plans. A plan may apply a 3-fill rule that moves long-term drugs to mail order after the third retail fill. When a claim rejects, the pharmacist reads the reject code (for example 75 for prior authorization required, or 76 for plan limits exceeded) and calls the plan or the prescriber's office.

## 6.1 CardioNex Patient Support Program
Halden Biopharma offers a patient support program, CardioNex Partners (fictional), to help patients start and stay on treatment. The program is independent of the prescriber's choice of treatment and does not require use of any other product. Services include:
- Benefit verification. A program specialist checks the patient's coverage, the tier, the cost share and whether prior authorization is required.
- Prior authorization support. The program supplies the plan's forms, a checklist of documents (LVEF report, potassium and eGFR, beta-blocker history) and, on request, a sample letter of medical necessity. The program does not complete clinical sections on behalf of the prescriber.
- Appeals support. The program explains the steps in 4.7 and the deadlines in the denial letter.
- Nurse educator line. A registered nurse is available to answer questions about taking the tablets, storage, and the importance of potassium and blood pressure checks, and does not give individual treatment advice. A caller who reports an adverse event is referred to the drug safety team, and the event is reported within 24 hours of first contact.
- Refill reminders by text or telephone, if the patient has agreed.

## 6.2 Copay Assistance and Financial Support
Commercially insured patients may be eligible for the CardioNex Savings Card, which lowers the out-of-pocket cost for a 30-day or 90-day supply, subject to an annual maximum. Eligibility terms:
- The patient must have commercial insurance that covers CardioNex. Patients with Medicare, Medicaid, TRICARE, VA or other government coverage are not eligible, in line with federal law.
- The card is not valid where prohibited by law or by the plan.
- The card is not health insurance and may be changed or ended at any time.
- The patient must have a valid prescription for an approved use.

Patients without insurance, or whose income is within the program's limits, may apply to the CardioNex Access Program for free product, subject to a prescriber's statement of need and proof of income. Medicare Part D patients may be referred to an independent charitable foundation and to the Medicare Extra Help program for low-income members. A transition fill program provides a limited free supply to commercially insured patients while a prior authorization request is pending, if the prior authorization was submitted and the plan has not responded within the times in 4.4.

## 6.3 Contact and Document Checklist
For the quickest decision, the prescriber's office should send, at the first request:
1. The completed plan prior authorization form, with the diagnosis code.
2. The LVEF report, dated within the past 12 months.
3. The date and value of the serum potassium (5.0 mEq/L or lower to start) and the eGFR.
4. The beta-blocker history: drug, dose, start date and stop date, and the reason for stopping, or the admission date if step therapy is waived.
5. A statement that the patient is not on an ACE inhibitor, not on dialysis and, for a patient who could become pregnant, is using effective contraception [cardionex-uspi-v2, 8.1].
6. The requested dose, the quantity (30 tablets per 30 days) and the planned titration.

A standard request is decided within 72 hours and an urgent request within 24 hours [see 4.4]. Questions about this guide, and requests for clinical information such as storage, missed doses, monitoring or switching from other drugs, are answered by the medical information team [see cardionex-medinfo-faq].
