# NEUROVEX (neurovexin) Package Insert, Japan, Tenant Pharma_B Confidential

## 0.1 Package Insert Header
- **Brand name:** NeuroVex Capsules 25 mg and 50 mg
- **Nonproprietary name:** neurovexin
- **Therapeutic category:** Agent for neuropathic pain from diabetic peripheral neuropathy
- **Standard commodity classification number of Japan:** 871149
- **Approval number:** 30800AMX00417 (25 mg) and 30800AMX00418 (50 mg)
- **Date of listing in the NHI drug price list:** June 2026
- **Date of initial marketing in Japan:** July 2026
- **Revision of this package insert:** July 2026 (1st edition)
- **Storage:** Store at room temperature (1 to 30 degrees C) in a tightly closed container. Prescription drug: use only on the prescription of a physician or dentist.

This package insert follows the format of the Pharmaceuticals and Medical Devices Agency (PMDA). The sections are given in the order of the Japanese package insert and refer to each other by number.

## 1.1 Approved use in Japan
Approved for neuropathic pain from diabetic peripheral neuropathy in adults.

## 1.2 Precautions Concerning Indications
- The diagnosis of diabetic peripheral neuropathy should be made by a physician with experience in diabetes care before NeuroVex is prescribed. Use NeuroVex only in patients with neuropathic pain that is attributable to diabetic peripheral neuropathy.
- The efficacy and safety of NeuroVex in patients with other kinds of neuropathic pain, such as postherpetic neuralgia or trigeminal neuralgia, have not been established in Japan, and NeuroVex has not been approved for these uses.
- The efficacy and safety in patients younger than 18 years have not been established.
- Judge whether the pain comes from a cause that needs a different treatment, such as a foot ulcer, an infection or a vascular disease, before starting NeuroVex.

## 2.1 Dosage in Japan
Start at **25mg twice daily** for 1 week, then raise to 50mg twice daily. Do not go above 100mg a day.

## 2.2 Renal impairment in Japan
- **Creatinine clearance 30 to 59 mL/min:** 25mg twice daily. Do not raise the dose.
- **Creatinine clearance 15 to 29 mL/min:** 25mg once daily.
- **Below 15 mL/min or on dialysis:** Do not use.

## 2.3 Stopping in Japan
Lower the dose to 25mg twice daily for at least 1 week before stopping.

## 2.4 Precautions Concerning Dosage
- **Creatinine clearance:** Neurovexin is excreted mainly by the kidneys, and the blood level rises as the creatinine clearance falls. Estimate the creatinine clearance, by the Cockcroft-Gault method, before starting treatment and choose the dose as in section 2.2.
- **Titration:** The start dose of 25mg twice daily for 1 week is given to improve tolerability. In a Japanese study, the rate of dizziness was lower when the dose was raised after 1 week than when treatment began at 50mg twice daily.
- **Maximum dose:** Doses above 100mg a day did not raise the efficacy and raised the rate of dizziness. Do not go above 100mg a day.
- **Stopping:** Lower the dose gradually as stated in section 2.3. Do not stop treatment suddenly [see 5.2].
- **Administration:** The capsules should be swallowed whole with water, in the morning and in the evening. They may be taken before or after meals.
- **Missed dose:** If a dose is missed, the patient should take the next dose at the usual time and should not take two doses at once.
- **Unresponsive patients:** If the pain has not got better after 12 weeks of treatment at the usual dose, consider whether to continue.

## 4.1 Contraindications in Japan
- Known allergy to neurovexin.
- Creatinine clearance below 15 mL/min, or dialysis.

## 4.2 Patients Requiring Careful Administration
The following patients should be given NeuroVex with care [see 5.2 and 8.5].
- Patients with a creatinine clearance below 60 mL/min (the blood level of neurovexin rises, and dizziness and sleepiness can occur more often) [see 2.2 and 8.6].
- Patients with heart failure (swelling and weight gain can occur).
- Patients with lung disease, including chronic obstructive pulmonary disease (slow or shallow breathing can occur).
- Patients who take an opioid, a benzodiazepine or another drug that slows the central nervous system, and patients who drink alcohol [see 7.1].
- Elderly patients [see 8.5].
- Patients with a history of drug abuse.

## 5.1 Driving in Japan
Patients must not drive or use machines while taking NeuroVex, because of dizziness and sleepiness.

## 5.2 Important Precautions
- Dizziness and sleepiness occur, in particular in the first 2 weeks of treatment. Tell patients that they must not drive a car or use machines that need attention, work at a height or do other dangerous work while they take NeuroVex [see 5.1].
- Swelling of the legs and feet and weight gain can occur. Watch the patient for these signs, in particular patients with heart failure, and stop treatment or lower the dose if they occur.
- If the patient's creatinine clearance falls during treatment, lower the dose as in section 2.2.
- Hypersensitivity reactions, including angioedema, can occur. Stop treatment and give suitable care if swelling of the face, lips, tongue or throat, or difficulty breathing, occurs.
- Stopping treatment suddenly can cause trouble sleeping, nausea, headache and anxiety. Lower the dose gradually [see 2.3].
- Because a patient's diabetes may need changes in care over time, tell the patient to continue the care of the diabetes, including the control of blood glucose, while taking NeuroVex.
- Thoughts of suicide have been reported in patients who take drugs with a similar action on nerve signaling. Watch patients for depressed mood and for suicidal thoughts.
- NeuroVex is not a drug that treats the cause of diabetic peripheral neuropathy. Control the underlying diabetes together with NeuroVex.

## 5.3 Precautions for Patients with Diabetic Complications
- Visual symptoms such as blurred vision and double vision were reported with NeuroVex. Because patients with diabetes can have retinopathy, a patient with a persistent visual symptom should be sent for an eye examination, and the symptom should not be taken for granted to be an adverse reaction of NeuroVex.
- Patients with a foot ulcer or an infection of the foot should be treated for it, because NeuroVex treats the pain and not the wound. A reduced pain may hide the signs of a new injury to the foot, and patients should check their feet every day.
- Dizziness and sleepiness may be taken for hypoglycemia, or may hide its early signs. Tell patients to check the blood glucose as advised.
- Many patients with diabetic peripheral neuropathy have a diabetic kidney disease. Estimate the creatinine clearance before the start of treatment and at least once a year [see 2.4].

## 5.4 Monitoring During Treatment
At each visit, ask the patient about dizziness, sleepiness, falls, swelling, weight change, breathing, visual symptoms and mood. Record the pain score and the interference of pain with sleep, so that the benefit of treatment can be judged. Check the creatinine clearance when the patient becomes ill or starts a drug that can reduce the kidney function. No routine blood test other than the creatinine clearance is needed for NeuroVex.

## 5.5 Precautions for Elderly Drivers and Patients at Risk of Falls
Elderly patients had dizziness more often than younger patients in the Japanese studies [see 9.3], and a fall can lead to a fracture. Tell elderly patients and their families about the risk of falls, in particular during the first 2 weeks and after any change in another drug that slows the central nervous system. Check that the patient can stand and walk safely at the visit after the start of treatment. Because the dose of 25mg twice daily for 1 week is meant to reduce dizziness, tell patients not to skip this first week of treatment and not to begin at the usual dose.

## 7.1 Drug Interactions (Precautions for Co-administration)
**Contraindicated co-administration:** none.

**Precautions for co-administration:**

| Drug | Sign, symptom and treatment | Mechanism and risk factor |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| Opioids (morphine, oxycodone and others) | Sleepiness, dizziness and slow or shallow breathing can be stronger. Start the added drug at a low dose and watch the patient closely | Both drugs slow the central nervous system |
| Benzodiazepines (lorazepam, diazepam and others) | Sleepiness, dizziness and slow or shallow breathing can be stronger. Start the added drug at a low dose and watch the patient closely | Both drugs slow the central nervous system |
| Alcohol | Sleepiness and dizziness can be stronger. Tell patients to avoid alcohol | Both slow the central nervous system |
| Other central nervous system depressants (sedating antihistamines, hypnotics, muscle relaxants) | Sleepiness can be stronger | Additive effect |
| Thiazolidinediones (pioglitazone) and calcium channel blockers (amlodipine) | Swelling of the legs and weight gain can be stronger | Additive effect on fluid retention |
| Cimetidine, probenecid | The blood level of neurovexin can rise by about 15% | Lower renal secretion |
| Aluminum and magnesium hydroxide antacids | The blood level of neurovexin can fall by about 20%. Take NeuroVex at least 2 hours after the antacid | Lower absorption |

Neurovexin is not metabolized by the liver in a relevant way and does not inhibit or induce the main cytochrome P450 enzymes. In studies in healthy adults, it did not change the exposure to metformin, glimepiride, atorvastatin or warfarin.

## 7.2 Results of Drug Interaction Studies
The following studies were done in healthy adults outside Japan and in Japanese adults.

| Co-administered drug | Effect on the AUC of neurovexin | Effect on the other drug |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| Oxycodone 10 mg single dose | No change | Cmax up 7%, AUC unchanged |
| Lorazepam 1 mg single dose | No change | No change |
| Cimetidine 400 mg twice daily | Up 15% | Not applicable |
| Probenecid 500 mg four times daily | Up 14% | Not applicable |
| Aluminum and magnesium hydroxide | Down 20% | Not applicable |
| Metformin 1,000 mg | No change | No change |
| Glimepiride 4 mg | No change | No change |
| Atorvastatin 40 mg | No change | No change |
| Warfarin 5 mg | No change | No change in INR |

The pharmacodynamic effect of neurovexin combined with lorazepam was a slowing of the reaction time of 14%, more than with either drug alone. With oxycodone 10 mg, the mean oxygen saturation fell by 2.1 percentage points more than with oxycodone alone.

## 8.1 Pregnancy in Japan
Give to a pregnant woman only if the benefit outweighs the risk.

## 8.2 Lactation
Neurovexin passes into human breast milk. Weigh the benefit of breastfeeding against the risk of sleepiness in the infant, and decide whether to continue breastfeeding or to stop NeuroVex.

## 8.3 Women Who May Become Pregnant
Ask women who may become pregnant to tell their physician if they plan to become pregnant. In animal studies, doses at least 8 times the human exposure caused delayed bone formation in the young [see 8.1].

## 8.4 Pediatric Use
The safety and efficacy of NeuroVex in patients younger than 18 years have not been established, because no clinical study has been done in this population.

## 8.5 Older patients in Japan
Start at 25mg twice daily and raise the dose only after checking kidney function.

## 8.6 Renal Impairment in Detail
Neurovexin is excreted mainly in the urine, and the half-life lengthens as the creatinine clearance falls: it was about 6 hours in Japanese volunteers with a creatinine clearance of 60 mL/min or more and about 12 hours in those with a creatinine clearance of 30 to 59 mL/min. Dizziness and sleepiness occur more often in these patients. Use the doses in section 2.2, and do not use NeuroVex in patients with a creatinine clearance below 15 mL/min or on dialysis [see 4.1].

## 8.7 Hepatic Impairment
No clinical study has been done in Japanese patients with hepatic impairment. In a study done outside Japan in patients with moderate hepatic impairment, the blood level of neurovexin was within 10% of that in healthy adults. Neurovexin is not metabolized in the liver in a relevant way. Choose the dose by creatinine clearance, as in section 2.2.

## 8.8 Dosage by Creatinine Clearance, Summary
| Creatinine clearance | Dose in Japan |
| :--- | :--- |
| 60 mL/min or more | 25mg twice daily for 1 week, then 50mg twice daily |
| 30 to 59 mL/min | 25mg twice daily, without a rise in the dose |
| 15 to 29 mL/min | 25mg once daily |
| Below 15 mL/min or on dialysis | Do not use |

The maximum dose is 100mg a day. This table repeats the doses in section 2.2 for convenience, and the doses in section 2.2 apply.

## 8.9 Patients Who Take Many Drugs
Patients with diabetic peripheral neuropathy often take drugs for blood glucose, blood pressure and blood lipids. NeuroVex did not change the exposure to metformin, glimepiride, atorvastatin or warfarin, and no change in their dose is needed when NeuroVex is added [see 7.1 and 7.2]. Review every drug that slows the central nervous system, and every drug that causes swelling of the legs, when the dose of NeuroVex is decided. Ask the patient about drugs bought without a prescription, such as sedating antihistamines for allergy or for sleep.

## 9.2 Clinically Significant Adverse Reactions
- **Slow or shallow breathing (frequency unknown):** Slow or shallow breathing can occur, in particular when NeuroVex is taken with an opioid, a benzodiazepine or alcohol, and in patients with lung disease. Watch the patient closely, and take suitable action, including a dose reduction or a stop of treatment, if it occurs.
- **Angioedema (frequency unknown):** Swelling of the face, lips, tongue or throat can occur. Stop treatment and give suitable care.
- **Loss of consciousness (under 0.1%):** Loss of consciousness can occur. Take suitable action, including a stop of treatment, if it occurs.
- **Rhabdomyolysis (frequency unknown):** Watch for muscle pain, weakness, a rise in creatine kinase and a rise in myoglobin in the blood and urine. Stop treatment if they are found.

## 10 Overdosage
Signs and symptoms: Sleepiness, confusion and agitation are the most likely effects of an overdose. Slurred speech and unsteady walking can occur.

Treatment: Give supportive care. Neurovexin is removed by hemodialysis, which takes out about half of the drug in 4 hours, and hemodialysis should be considered in a patient with severe signs or a reduced creatinine clearance. If the patient has also taken an opioid, a benzodiazepine or alcohol, watch the breathing closely.

## 11.1 Precautions for Application
- **At the time of dispensing:** Dispense NeuroVex in the original container. Tell the patient to take the capsule out of the container just before taking it.
- **At the time of taking:** Tell the patient to swallow the capsule whole, without opening, crushing or chewing it.
- **In patients with diabetes:** Tell the patient to continue to check the blood glucose as the diabetes care team has advised.

## 11.2 Other Precautions
- In a study in rats for 104 weeks, there was no increase in the rate of tumors at doses up to 10 times the human exposure at the maximum clinical dose. In a study in mice for 104 weeks, there was none at doses up to 14 times the human exposure.
- Neurovexin was not mutagenic in the bacterial reverse mutation assay and the mouse lymphoma assay, and did not cause chromosome damage in the rat micronucleus test.
- In animal studies, doses of neurovexin at 25 times the human exposure caused a reversible change in the renal tubules of rats.

## 12.1 Pharmacokinetics in Japanese Subjects
**Single dose.** After a single oral dose of NeuroVex 50 mg to 12 healthy Japanese adult men on an empty stomach, the maximum blood level was reached in about 1 hour. The half-life was about 6 hours. About 90% of the dose was excreted unchanged in urine within 48 hours.

**Repeated doses.** After 50mg twice daily for 7 days in 12 healthy Japanese adult men, the steady state was reached within 2 days and the accumulation ratio was 1.3.

| Parameter | Single 50 mg dose (n=12) | 50 mg twice daily, day 7 (n=12) |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| Cmax (ng/mL) | 1,720 (26) | 2,180 (23) |
| Tmax, median (range), h | 1.0 (0.5 to 1.5) | 1.0 (0.5 to 2.0) |
| AUC (ng.h/mL) | 8,350 (21) over 0 to infinity | 8,040 (20) over 12 hours |
| Half-life (h) | about 6 | about 6 |
| Urinary excretion (% of dose) | about 90 | about 90 |

Values are the mean, with the percent coefficient of variation in parentheses. The exposure in Japanese adults was within 10% of that in non-Japanese adults after adjusting for body weight and creatinine clearance.

## 12.2 Effect of Food
After a single 50 mg dose with a high-fat meal, the time to the maximum blood level was delayed from about 1 hour to about 2 hours, the Cmax fell by about 9% and the AUC did not change. NeuroVex may therefore be taken with or without meals.

## 12.3 Renal Impairment
In Japanese adults who took a single 50 mg dose, the half-life and the exposure rose as the creatinine clearance fell.

| Creatinine clearance | Number | AUC ratio to normal | Half-life (h) |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| 60 mL/min or more | 8 | 1.0 | about 6 |
| 30 to 59 mL/min | 8 | 2.0 | about 12 |
| 15 to 29 mL/min | 6 | 4.0 | about 24 |

The doses in section 2.2 were chosen to keep the exposure near that of a patient with a normal kidney function. Hemodialysis removes about half of the drug in 4 hours.

## 12.4 Distribution, Metabolism and Excretion
- **Protein binding:** Less than 3% of neurovexin is bound to plasma proteins in vitro.
- **Metabolism:** Neurovexin is not metabolized in the liver in a relevant way. Less than 3% of the dose is found in urine as metabolites.
- **Excretion:** About 90% of the dose is excreted unchanged in urine. The renal clearance was close to the creatinine clearance.
- **Breast milk:** Neurovexin passes into human breast milk.

## 12.5 Population Pharmacokinetics
In an analysis of the data from the Japanese and the global studies, the creatinine clearance was the only factor that explained a clinically relevant part of the difference in the exposure to neurovexin between patients. Age, sex, body weight, race and the use of insulin did not change the exposure by more than 10% after adjustment for the creatinine clearance. The apparent oral clearance was about 6.3 L/h in a patient with a normal kidney function and fell in line with the creatinine clearance.

## 13.1 Japanese Phase 3 Study (NB-JP-301)
A 12-week, randomized, double-blind, placebo-controlled study was done at 41 sites in Japan in 312 adult patients with painful diabetic peripheral neuropathy of at least 6 months and an average daily pain score of 4 or more on a 0 to 10 scale. Patients were randomized 1 to 1 to NeuroVex or placebo. NeuroVex was started at 25mg twice daily for 1 week and raised to 50mg twice daily from week 2. Patients with a creatinine clearance below 30 mL/min were excluded. The primary endpoint was the change in the mean daily pain score from the start to week 12.

| Outcome at week 12 | NeuroVex (n=156) | Placebo (n=156) | Difference (95% CI) |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| Mean drop in daily pain score (points) | 2.2 | 1.0 | 1.2 (0.6 to 1.8) |
| At least 30% less pain (%) | 45 | 28 | 17 (7 to 27) |
| At least 50% less pain (%) | 26 | 14 | 12 (3 to 21) |

The difference between the arms in the primary endpoint was statistically significant (p<0.001). Dizziness led to stopping in 7.7% of the patients on NeuroVex and 2.6% of the patients on placebo.

## 13.2 Japanese Long-Term Study
In a 52-week, open-label study in 150 Japanese patients, NeuroVex was started at 25mg twice daily for 1 week and raised to 50mg twice daily, or kept at a lower dose according to the creatinine clearance. The mean daily pain score fell from 6.4 at the start to 3.8 at week 52. Adverse reactions were reported in 52.0% of the patients, and the main ones were dizziness, sleepiness, swelling of the legs and weight gain. No new kind of adverse reaction was seen.

## 13.3 Global Phase 3 Study (NEURO-BRIDGE)
In the 12-week global study NEURO-BRIDGE in 1,260 adults, the mean daily pain score fell by 2.4 points on NeuroVex 50mg twice daily and by 1.1 points on placebo (difference 1.3, 95% CI 0.9 to 1.7). In this study, 8.2% of the patients on NeuroVex and 3.1% on placebo stopped treatment because of dizziness. The dosing in the global study did not include the 1-week start dose of 25mg twice daily that applies in Japan.

## 13.4 Results of NB-JP-301 by Age
In the Japanese phase 3 study, 44% of the patients were 65 years or older. The drop in the daily pain score at week 12 was 2.2 points on NeuroVex and 1.0 points on placebo in patients under 65 years, and 2.1 points and 1.0 points in patients 65 years or older. Dizziness was reported in 20.5% of the patients 65 years or older and 12.1% of those under 65 years on NeuroVex. These results agree with those of the global study NEURO-BRIDGE, in which dizziness was also more common in patients 65 years or older.

## 13.5 Efficacy in Sleep in the Japanese Study
In NB-JP-301, the daily sleep interference score, on a 0 to 10 scale on which a higher score means that pain interferes more with sleep, fell by 1.9 points on NeuroVex and 0.8 points on placebo at week 12 (difference 1.1, 95% CI 0.5 to 1.7). The patient global impression of change was rated "much improved" or "very much improved" by 42% of the patients on NeuroVex and 27% of those on placebo. A rescue analgesic, limited to paracetamol, was used on a mean of 2.0 days a week on NeuroVex and 2.7 days a week on placebo.

## 14 Pharmacology
Neurovexin binds with high affinity to an auxiliary subunit of voltage-gated calcium channels in the nerves that carry pain signals, and lowers the release of excitatory neurotransmitters in the spinal cord. In animal models of diabetic neuropathy and of nerve injury, neurovexin reduced the response to touch and heat that would normally not cause pain. It does not bind to opioid, dopamine, serotonin or GABA receptors. The effect on pain does not depend on a change in blood glucose.

## 14.2 Pharmacodynamics
In a thorough QT study in healthy adults, single doses of neurovexin 100 mg and 400 mg did not lengthen the QT interval, and the upper bound of the 90% confidence interval of the change corrected for the heart rate was below 10 ms. In healthy adults, neurovexin 100 mg slowed the reaction time on a computer-based test by 6% compared with placebo, with the largest effect 1 to 2 hours after the dose.
