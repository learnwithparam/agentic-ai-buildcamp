This document summarizes the design, conduct and results of the CLARION-HF trial. The study supports the indication to reduce the risk of cardiovascular death and hospitalization for heart failure in adults with chronic heart failure with reduced ejection fraction (HFrEF, LVEF 40% or lower), as described in the Prescribing Information (cardionex-uspi-v2, section 1.1). Section numbers in this summary follow the order of a clinical study report: identifiers and objectives (1), methods (2), efficacy results (3), safety results (4) and interpretation (5).

## 1.2 Sponsor, Oversight and Committees
CLARION-HF was designed and funded by the sponsor. An executive steering committee of academic cardiologists, nephrologists and one biostatistician oversaw the design, the conduct and the interpretation of the trial. The committee had final authority over the protocol and the statistical analysis plan, and the sponsor had the right to comment on the manuscript but not to veto its content.

An independent data monitoring committee (IDMC) of five members, none of them employed by the sponsor, reviewed unblinded safety data every 6 months and efficacy data at two prespecified interim analyses. The IDMC charter allowed the committee to recommend stopping for overwhelming efficacy only at a nominal two-sided p-value below 0.001 for the primary endpoint at the second interim analysis, and to recommend stopping for harm at any review. The committee recommended that the trial continue unchanged after each review.

A clinical events committee (CEC) of cardiologists who were blinded to treatment assignment adjudicated every death, every hospitalization for heart failure, every episode of angioedema and every case of potassium above 6.0 mEq/L, using a written charter. A hospitalization for heart failure was defined as an unplanned admission of at least 24 hours with worsening symptoms or signs of heart failure and treatment with intravenous diuretic, vasodilator or inotrope therapy, or with mechanical or surgical intervention. Deaths were classified as cardiovascular unless a clear non-cardiovascular cause was identified.

A central laboratory performed all protocol-specified serum potassium, creatinine, eGFR and NT-proBNP measurements, so that results were comparable across sites and regions. Site investigators received the potassium result within 24 hours of sample receipt for the safety visits described in the dose adjustment rules [see Treatments and Titration (2.5)].

## 1.4 Study Objectives
The primary objective was to show that CardioNex, added to usual care, reduces the time to the first occurrence of cardiovascular death or hospitalization for heart failure compared with placebo in adults with chronic HFrEF.

The key secondary objectives were to compare the two arms on cardiovascular death, on the first hospitalization for heart failure and on death from any cause, in the order given in the testing hierarchy [see Statistical Methods (2.8)].

The safety objectives were to characterize the incidence of hyperkalemia, symptomatic hypotension, worsening renal function and angioedema, to describe adverse events and serious adverse events overall, and to describe the effect of baseline kidney function and of concomitant potassium-raising drugs on these events [see Potassium and Kidney Results (3.4)].

The exploratory objectives included effects in prespecified subgroups, change in the Kansas City Cardiomyopathy Questionnaire clinical summary score (KCCQ-CSS) at 8 months, change in NT-proBNP, and the total number of heart failure hospitalizations including repeat events.

## 2.1 Design
Adults with chronic heart failure, an LVEF of 40% or lower and an eGFR of 30 mL/min or more were randomized 1 to 1 to CardioNex (20mg once daily, raised to 40mg after 4 weeks) or placebo, on top of usual care. The median follow-up was 26 months. The primary outcome was the first of cardiovascular death or hospitalization for heart failure.

## 2.2 Study Design Details
CLARION-HF was a multicenter, randomized, double-blind, placebo-controlled, parallel-group, event-driven trial. After a screening visit and a single-blind placebo run-in of 2 weeks, eligible patients were randomized 1 to 1 using a central interactive web response system. Randomization was stratified by region, by baseline eGFR (30 to 59 vs 60 mL/min/1.73 m2 or higher) and by whether the patient had been hospitalized for heart failure in the previous 12 months, with permuted blocks within each stratum.

Patients, investigators, the sponsor's study team, the central laboratory and the clinical events committee were blinded to treatment assignment until database lock. Active tablets and placebo tablets were identical in size, color, taste and packaging within each strength (5 mg, 20 mg and 40 mg). To protect the blind, the central laboratory reported potassium results to sites as a flag (within the protocol range, above the withholding threshold) rather than as a treatment-linked value, and the blinded dose-adjustment rules applied equally to both arms.

The trial was event-driven. Enrolment continued, and follow-up continued, until 802 adjudicated primary endpoint events had accrued. Patients who stopped study treatment early remained in the trial and were followed for all endpoints, so the primary analysis followed the intention-to-treat principle.

Visits took place at randomization, week 1, week 2, week 4, week 5, week 8, month 3 and then every 3 months, with a final visit after treatment ended. Blood pressure, heart rate, weight, serum potassium and creatinine were measured at each visit in the first 3 months. The KCCQ was completed at randomization, month 4, month 8 and month 12.

## 2.3 Inclusion Criteria
Patients were eligible if all of the following applied at screening:
- Age 18 years or older.
- A clinical diagnosis of chronic heart failure for at least 3 months, in New York Heart Association (NYHA) functional class II, III or IV.
- A left ventricular ejection fraction (LVEF) of 40% or lower on echocardiography, radionuclide imaging or magnetic resonance imaging within the previous 12 months.
- An estimated glomerular filtration rate (eGFR) of 30 mL/min/1.73 m2 or higher by the CKD-EPI creatinine equation at screening.
- Serum potassium at or below 5.2 mEq/L at screening, as measured by the central laboratory.
- NT-proBNP of at least 600 pg/mL (or at least 400 pg/mL if hospitalized for heart failure within 12 months; at least 900 pg/mL if in atrial fibrillation).
- Treatment with a stable dose of a beta-blocker for at least 4 weeks, unless the beta-blocker was not tolerated or contraindicated, and with a diuretic when clinically needed.
- Systolic blood pressure of at least 100 mmHg at screening and at randomization.
- Written informed consent.

## 2.4 Exclusion Criteria
Patients were excluded if any of the following applied:
- Treatment with an ACE inhibitor within 36 hours before randomization, or a history of angioedema of any cause.
- Treatment with an angiotensin receptor blocker or sacubitril and valsartan that could not be stopped at randomization.
- Dialysis, a kidney transplant, or an eGFR below 30 mL/min/1.73 m2 at screening.
- Serum potassium above 5.2 mEq/L at screening, or a history of potassium above 6.0 mEq/L while on a renin-angiotensin system drug.
- Heart failure with an LVEF above 40%, or heart failure caused by restrictive or infiltrative cardiomyopathy, constrictive pericarditis, hypertrophic obstructive cardiomyopathy or uncorrected severe valvular disease.
- Acute coronary syndrome, stroke, coronary revascularization or cardiac surgery within the previous 3 months.
- Symptomatic hypotension or systolic blood pressure below 100 mmHg.
- Severe hepatic impairment (Child-Pugh class C) or alanine aminotransferase more than 3 times the upper limit of normal.
- Current use of a strong CYP3A4 inhibitor (itraconazole, ketoconazole, clarithromycin or ritonavir) or a strong CYP3A4 inducer (rifampin, carbamazepine, phenytoin or St John's wort).
- Pregnancy or breastfeeding, or a woman of childbearing potential not using effective contraception.
- A life expectancy under 12 months from a non-cardiovascular condition.

## 2.5 Treatments and Titration
Eligible patients received CardioNex 20 mg or matching placebo once daily at about the same time each day, with or without food. After 4 weeks, patients who met the titration conditions were raised to 40 mg or matching placebo once daily, which was the target dose.

| Visit | Action |
| :--- | :--- |
| Randomization | Start 20 mg (or placebo). Central laboratory potassium and creatinine at baseline |
| Week 1 | Check potassium and blood pressure. Continue if potassium is 5.5 mEq/L or lower |
| Week 2 | Check creatinine and potassium |
| Week 4 | Raise to 40 mg if potassium is 5.0 mEq/L or lower, systolic blood pressure is at least 100 mmHg and eGFR has not fallen by more than 30% from baseline |
| Week 5 | Check potassium, creatinine and blood pressure after the dose change |
| Month 2 and month 3 | Check potassium |

Study treatment was withheld while serum potassium was above 5.5 mEq/L and restarted at the previous dose only once potassium had returned to the normal range. Study treatment was reduced or interrupted for symptomatic hypotension after the diuretic dose had been lowered first, in line with the Prescribing Information [see Warnings and Precautions (5.1) and (5.2) in cardionex-uspi-v2]. Treatment was stopped permanently, and not restarted, for angioedema. No dose above 40 mg once daily was used in the trial.

At the end of titration, 87.4% of patients on CardioNex and 89.0% of patients on placebo were taking the target dose of 40 mg once daily. The mean treatment adherence by tablet count was 94% in both arms.

## 2.6 Concomitant Therapy
Patients continued usual care for chronic heart failure throughout the trial. Investigators were asked to keep background drugs at stable doses for the first 3 months, except as needed for safety.
- Beta-blockers were permitted and were taken by 93.6% of the patients at baseline.
- Mineralocorticoid receptor antagonists (spironolactone or eplerenone) were permitted if potassium was 5.0 mEq/L or lower, with a protocol-required potassium check at each study visit. Spironolactone was taken by 31.8% of patients at baseline.
- Sodium-glucose cotransporter 2 (SGLT2) inhibitors were permitted and were taken by 19.4% of patients at baseline. The proportion rose to 27.5% by the end of the trial because background guideline therapy changed during the study period.
- Loop diuretics were adjusted freely by the investigator to maintain euvolemia.
- ACE inhibitors, angiotensin receptor blockers and sacubitril and valsartan were prohibited during study treatment. Potassium supplements, potassium-containing salt substitutes and strong CYP3A4 inhibitors or inducers were prohibited, and trial participants were counseled to avoid them.
- Nonsteroidal anti-inflammatory drugs were discouraged but not prohibited.

## 2.7 Endpoints
The primary endpoint was the time to the first adjudicated occurrence of cardiovascular death or hospitalization for heart failure.

The key secondary endpoints, tested in a fixed order, were:
- Time to cardiovascular death.
- Time to first hospitalization for heart failure.
- Time to death from any cause.

Other prespecified endpoints included the total (first and recurrent) number of hospitalizations for heart failure and cardiovascular death, the change from baseline to month 8 in the KCCQ-CSS, the change from baseline in NT-proBNP at month 4, the proportion of patients with a decline in eGFR of 40% or more, and the time to a first episode of worsening NYHA class.

Safety endpoints were adverse events, serious adverse events, adverse events leading to treatment discontinuation, the incidence of serum potassium above 5.5 mEq/L and above 6.0 mEq/L, symptomatic hypotension, worsening renal function (a rise in serum creatinine of more than 30% from baseline, or as reported by the investigator), angioedema, and laboratory and vital sign abnormalities.

## 2.8 Statistical Methods
Sample size. The trial was planned for 90% power at a two-sided significance level of 0.05 to detect a hazard ratio of 0.80 for the primary endpoint, which required 802 primary events. Assuming an annual placebo event rate of 8.5 per 100 patient-years, an enrolment period of 18 months and a minimum follow-up of 12 months, the sample size was set at 4,800 patients, and 4,820 were randomized.

Analysis populations. The intention-to-treat (ITT) population included all randomized patients analyzed according to their assigned treatment. The safety population included all randomized patients who took at least one dose of study treatment, analyzed according to treatment received. Safety results below are reported for the safety population (CardioNex n=2,408, placebo n=2,410).

Primary analysis. The primary endpoint was analyzed in the ITT population using a Cox proportional-hazards model with treatment as the factor, stratified by region and by prior hospitalization for heart failure, and adjusted for baseline eGFR band. The hazard ratio, its 95% confidence interval and a two-sided p-value from the stratified log-rank test were reported. The proportional-hazards assumption was checked with scaled Schoenfeld residuals and by a time-by-treatment interaction term; neither test was significant. Patients without an event were censored at the last date they were known to be alive and event free, or at the end of the trial.

Secondary and exploratory analyses. The same Cox model was used for each key secondary endpoint. Recurrent hospitalizations were analyzed with a joint frailty model. The KCCQ-CSS was analyzed with a mixed model for repeated measures. Subgroup effects were estimated with the same model within each subgroup, and a treatment-by-subgroup interaction test was done for each variable without adjustment for multiple testing.

Interim analyses. Two interim analyses were planned, after about 50% and 75% of the primary events, using the Lan-DeMets O'Brien-Fleming spending function, so that the final analysis was tested at a two-sided level close to 0.049.

Missing data. Vital status was ascertained for 99.2% of patients at the end of the trial, including by public record searches where the country allowed it. Missing laboratory values were not imputed in the safety analyses.

## 2.9 Testing Hierarchy and Control of Error Rates
To control the overall type I error rate at a two-sided 0.05, the primary endpoint and the three key secondary endpoints were tested in a fixed sequence, each at the full level, and testing stopped at the first endpoint that did not reach statistical significance:
1. Primary composite of cardiovascular death or first hospitalization for heart failure.
2. Cardiovascular death.
3. First hospitalization for heart failure.
4. Death from any cause.

The primary composite, cardiovascular death and first hospitalization for heart failure each reached significance [see Primary and Subgroup Efficacy Outcomes (3.1) and Secondary Outcomes (3.3)]. For death from any cause, the point estimate favored CardioNex, with a hazard ratio of 0.86, but the upper bound of the 95% confidence interval was 1.00, so the endpoint is described as a favorable trend and not as a formally confirmed effect. All other analyses, including subgroup, safety and exploratory analyses, were not adjusted for multiplicity, and their p-values and confidence intervals should be read as descriptive.

## 3.7 Time-to-Event Description
The Kaplan-Meier curves for the primary endpoint separated at about 3 months and continued to diverge during follow-up. The cumulative incidence of the primary endpoint at 12 months was 8.1% on CardioNex and 11.4% on placebo, and at 24 months it was 12.6% on CardioNex and 17.3% on placebo. The number of primary events was 342 in the CardioNex arm and 460 in the placebo arm (802 in total, the number required by the protocol). The number needed to treat to prevent one primary event over the median follow-up of 26 months was 20 (95% CI 15 to 31).

Sensitivity analyses gave results consistent with the primary analysis: the hazard ratio was 0.73 (95% CI 0.64 to 0.83) in the per-protocol population, 0.75 (95% CI 0.66 to 0.85) in an unstratified Cox model, and 0.74 (95% CI 0.65 to 0.85) when the 38 patients with unknown vital status near the end of the trial were counted as having a cardiovascular death in the CardioNex arm only.

## 4.6 Renal Function and Laboratory Findings
Worsening renal function was reported in 4.2% of patients on CardioNex and 3.8% on placebo. Mean serum creatinine rose by 0.07 mg/dL in the first 4 weeks on CardioNex and by 0.02 mg/dL on placebo, and it did not rise further. A rise in serum creatinine of more than 30% from baseline at any visit occurred in 7.3% of patients on CardioNex and 4.9% on placebo; it led to a review of the diuretic dose and of kidney-active drugs in most cases [see Worsening Renal Function (5.4) in cardionex-uspi-v2].

Liver tests did not differ between arms. An alanine aminotransferase level above 3 times the upper limit of normal occurred in 1.1% of patients on CardioNex and 1.0% on placebo. No patient met the criteria for drug-induced liver injury. Mean hemoglobin, sodium and uric acid values did not change by more than the laboratory variation seen on placebo.

## 4.7 Angioedema
Angioedema was reported by 10 patients (0.4%) on CardioNex and 2 patients (0.1%) on placebo. All 12 episodes were reviewed by the clinical events committee. The median time from the first dose to onset was 47 days (range 3 to 361 days). Of the 10 patients on CardioNex, 6 were of Black or African American or other non-White race, and 2 had a history of an allergic disorder; none had a history of angioedema, because this was an exclusion criterion. Patients who have had angioedema of any cause should not receive CardioNex [see Angioedema (5.3) in cardionex-uspi-v2].

## 5.1 Limitations
- The trial enrolled patients with an eGFR of 30 mL/min/1.73 m2 or higher and a potassium of 5.2 mEq/L or lower, after a placebo run-in that removed patients with early potassium or blood pressure problems. Results may therefore underestimate how often hyperkalemia and hypotension occur in routine care.
- Dose levels above 40 mg once daily and a lower target dose were not tested. The 5 mg dose used in labeling for severe renal impairment (eGFR below 30 mL/min, non-dialysis) and with strong CYP3A4 inhibitors rests on pharmacokinetic data and not on a randomized comparison.
- Fewer than 20% of patients took an SGLT2 inhibitor at baseline. The trial was not designed to test whether the effect of CardioNex differs by SGLT2 inhibitor use, and the subgroup was too small for a reliable estimate.
- Only 26% of patients were women and 14% were 75 or older, so confidence intervals for these subgroups are wide.
- Subgroup, exploratory and safety results were not adjusted for multiplicity.
- Death from any cause did not reach statistical significance in the fixed hierarchy, and the result for this endpoint should be described as a favorable trend.
- The median follow-up was 26 months. Long-term safety, including the incidence of angioedema over many years, is not established by this trial.
- Heart failure with preserved ejection fraction (LVEF above 40%), pediatric patients, pregnant women and patients on dialysis were not studied.

## 5.2 Conclusions
In adults with chronic heart failure and an LVEF of 40% or lower, CardioNex added to usual care reduced the risk of cardiovascular death or first hospitalization for heart failure by 26% compared with placebo (hazard ratio 0.74, 95% CI 0.65 to 0.84, p < 0.001) over a median follow-up of 26 months. The result was driven by fewer hospitalizations for heart failure (hazard ratio 0.71) and fewer cardiovascular deaths (hazard ratio 0.79), and it was consistent across subgroups defined by age, sex, region, kidney function, LVEF, diabetes and baseline potassium.

The treatment was associated with more symptomatic hypotension (9.1% vs 4.5%), more hyperkalemia (6.8% vs 3.4%) and more angioedema (0.4% vs 0.1%) than placebo, and with a small, early and non-progressive rise in creatinine. These risks are managed by potassium and blood pressure monitoring, by not initiating treatment when baseline potassium exceeds 5.0 mEq/L, and by stopping and not restarting treatment after angioedema.
