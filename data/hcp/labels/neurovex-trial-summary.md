## 1.2 Objectives
The primary objective was to show that NeuroVex 50mg twice daily lowers the daily pain score more than placebo after 12 weeks in adults with painful diabetic peripheral neuropathy. The secondary objectives were to compare the arms in the proportion of patients with at least 30% and at least 50% less pain, in the interference of pain with sleep, in the patient's global impression of change and in the use of rescue pain medicine, and to describe the safety and the tolerability of NeuroVex, in particular in patients 65 years or older. An exploratory objective was to describe the effect of kidney function on the results and to describe the plasma level of neurovexin in a subset of patients.

## 1.3 Trial Organization
The trial was done in agreement with the principles of Good Clinical Practice and the Declaration of Helsinki. The protocol and the informed consent forms were approved by an ethics committee at each site, and every patient gave written informed consent before any trial procedure. An independent data monitoring committee of two physicians and one statistician reviewed the unblinded safety data every 3 months and did not ask for a change in the trial. The sponsor and the investigators were blinded to the arm until the database was locked. Capsules of NeuroVex and placebo were identical in size, color and taste.

## 2.1 Design
Adults with painful diabetic peripheral neuropathy for at least 6 months and an average daily pain score of 4 or more on a 0 to 10 scale. Patients were randomized 1 to 1 to NeuroVex 50mg twice daily or placebo for 12 weeks. Patients younger than 18 and patients with a creatinine clearance below 30 mL/min were excluded.

## 2.2 Eligibility Criteria
**Inclusion criteria**
- Men and women aged 18 years or older.
- Type 1 or type 2 diabetes mellitus, with a stable regimen of diabetes treatment for at least 3 months and a hemoglobin A1c of 11% or less at screening.
- A diagnosis of painful diabetic peripheral neuropathy for at least 6 months, confirmed by a distal symmetric sensorimotor pattern on examination and a score of 3 or more on a neuropathy screening instrument.
- An average daily pain score of 4 or more on a 0 to 10 scale over the 7 days of the baseline period, with a daily diary completed on at least 5 of those 7 days.
- A creatinine clearance of 30 mL/min or more.

**Exclusion criteria**
- Patients younger than 18 years.
- A creatinine clearance below 30 mL/min.
- Other pain that could confuse the assessment of the pain from neuropathy, such as fibromyalgia, a severe foot ulcer or an inflammatory arthritis.
- Previous treatment with neurovexin, or no response to an adequate course of a drug with a similar mechanism.
- New York Heart Association class III or IV heart failure, a myocardial infarction or a stroke in the last 6 months, or an uncontrolled arrhythmia.
- Uncontrolled seizures, a history of suicide attempt, or a current major depressive episode with suicidal thoughts.
- Chronic use of an opioid at a dose above 30 mg of oral morphine equivalents a day, or use of a benzodiazepine for a sleep disorder at a dose that the investigator could not keep stable. Patients on a stable, lower dose of an opioid were allowed.
- Drug or alcohol abuse in the last 12 months, severe lung disease needing home oxygen, and an alanine aminotransferase above 3 times the upper limit of normal.
- Pregnancy or breastfeeding, and a woman who could become pregnant and did not use an accepted method of birth control.

## 2.3 Treatments and Randomization
Eligible patients were randomized 1 to 1 to NeuroVex 50mg twice daily or matching placebo twice daily, for 12 weeks. The dose was not titrated: the patients took 50mg twice daily from the first day, with or without food. Randomization was done by a central interactive system in blocks of 4, stratified by region and by the use of insulin at baseline. Patients could take paracetamol (acetaminophen) up to 3 g a day as rescue pain medicine and recorded each day on which they took it. A stable dose of a drug for neuropathic pain other than neurovexin was not allowed, and these drugs had to be stopped at least 2 weeks before the baseline period. Patients who stopped the study drug early were asked to attend all later visits when they could.

## 2.4 Endpoints
**Primary endpoint:** the change from baseline to week 12 in the mean daily pain score. Patients rated the average pain in the last 24 hours each evening on a 0 to 10 scale on which 0 was no pain and 10 was the worst pain imaginable, and the mean of the last 7 days before each visit was used.

**Key secondary endpoints**, tested in this fixed order:
1. The proportion of patients with at least 30% less pain at week 12.
2. The proportion of patients with at least 50% less pain at week 12.
3. The change from baseline to week 12 in the mean daily sleep interference score, on a 0 to 10 scale on which a higher score means that pain interferes more with sleep.

**Other secondary endpoints:** the patient global impression of change at week 12, the days of rescue pain medicine a week, and the change in the daily pain score at each week.

**Safety endpoints:** adverse events, with a focus on dizziness, sleepiness, swelling, and breathing and visual events, serious adverse events, stopping because of an adverse event, laboratory values, vital signs, weight and the electrocardiogram.

## 2.5 Statistical Methods
The primary analysis used a mixed model for repeated measures on the change from baseline in the weekly mean daily pain score, with treatment, week, the baseline pain score, the baseline score by week interaction, the use of insulin and a group of study sites as terms, and an unstructured covariance. The treatment difference at week 12 was reported as a least-squares mean difference with a 95% confidence interval, and the two-sided significance level was 0.05. The model assumed that missing data were missing at random.

The responder analyses used a logistic regression model with the same covariates. A patient who stopped treatment early, or who had no week 12 value, was counted as a non-responder. The difference in the proportions was reported with a 95% confidence interval by the Wald method with a stratified adjustment. The sleep interference score was analyzed with the same model as the primary endpoint.

The three key secondary endpoints were tested in a fixed sequence, and each was tested at the 0.05 level only if the endpoint before it had been significant. The subgroup analyses and the other secondary endpoints were not adjusted for multiple testing, and no p-value from them is a confirmatory result.

## 2.6 Sample Size
A sample of 336 patients in each arm gave 90% power at the two-sided 0.05 level to detect a difference of 0.8 points in the change in the mean daily pain score, assuming a standard deviation of 3.2. The sample was raised to 630 patients in each arm, a total of 1,260 patients, to give a safety database large enough to see an adverse reaction that occurs in 1 patient in 200 with a probability above 95%, and to give enough patients 65 years or older for a description of the safety and the efficacy in older patients, in whom about 40% of the patients were expected to be.

## 2.7 Analysis Populations
- **Randomized population:** all 1,260 patients who were randomized.
- **Full analysis population:** all randomized patients who took at least one dose of study drug and had at least one post-baseline pain score. This population was used for the efficacy analyses.
- **Safety population:** all patients who took at least one dose of study drug, analyzed by the treatment received. There were 630 patients in each arm, and all patients took the arm to which they were randomized.
- **Per-protocol population:** patients in the full analysis population without a major protocol deviation, used for a sensitivity analysis.

- **Dizziness leading to stopping treatment:** 8.2% on NeuroVex versus 3.1% on placebo.
- **Pain score drop at week 12:** 2.4 points on NeuroVex versus 1.1 points on placebo.

## 3.3 Adverse reactions
Adverse reactions in at least 5% of patients and more often than placebo were dizziness (14.1% vs 4.8%), sleepiness (9.6% vs 2.9%), leg swelling (6.0% vs 2.2%) and dry mouth (5.2% vs 1.9%). Serious adverse events occurred in 3.0% on NeuroVex and 2.7% on placebo.

## 3.4 Older patients
Of the patients in the trial, 41% were 65 or older. The pain score drop was the same as in younger patients. Dizziness was more common at 65 or older (18.0% vs 11.4% under 65).

## 3.5 Primary Endpoint in Detail
The least-squares mean drop in the daily pain score from baseline to week 12 was 2.4 points on NeuroVex (standard error 0.15) and 1.1 points on placebo (standard error 0.15). The difference was 1.3 points (95% CI 0.9 to 1.7, p<0.001). In terms of the 0 to 10 scale, the mean score at week 12 was about 4.1 on NeuroVex and 5.4 on placebo, from a baseline of 6.5 in both arms. The effect size, the difference divided by the pooled standard deviation of the change, was 0.4.

The result of the primary analysis in the per-protocol population was the same as in the full analysis population: a difference of 1.4 points (95% CI 1.0 to 1.8).

## 3.7 Responder Analyses
The proportion of patients with at least 30% less pain at week 12 was 47% on NeuroVex and 29% on placebo, a difference of 18 percentage points (95% CI 13% to 23%, p<0.001). The odds ratio was 2.17 (95% CI 1.72 to 2.74). The proportion with at least 50% less pain was 28% on NeuroVex and 15% on placebo, a difference of 13 percentage points (95% CI 9% to 17%, p<0.001), and the odds ratio was 2.20 (95% CI 1.66 to 2.91). The number of patients who need to be treated for one more patient to reach at least 30% less pain was 6 (95% CI 5 to 8), and for at least 50% less pain it was 8 (95% CI 6 to 12).

## 3.8 Sleep, Global Impression and Rescue Medicine
The mean drop in the daily sleep interference score at week 12 was 2.1 points on NeuroVex and 0.9 points on placebo, a difference of 1.2 points (95% CI 0.8 to 1.6, p<0.001). The third key secondary endpoint was therefore met, and all three key secondary endpoints were significant in the fixed order.

The patient global impression of change was rated "much improved" or "very much improved" by 44% of the patients on NeuroVex and 28% on placebo. Rescue pain medicine, limited to paracetamol (acetaminophen), was used on a mean of 1.9 days a week on NeuroVex and 2.6 days a week on placebo. The mean change in a quality-of-life score and in a depression scale was small and did not differ from placebo.

## 3.12 Discontinuations, Serious Events and Deaths
The adverse reactions that most often led to stopping the study drug were dizziness (8.2% on NeuroVex and 3.1% on placebo), sleepiness (2.5% and 0.8%), leg swelling (1.0% and 0.3%) and unsteady walking (0.6% and 0.2%). Any adverse event led to stopping in 12.4% of the patients on NeuroVex and 6.0% on placebo.

Serious adverse events occurred in 3.0% of the patients on NeuroVex and 2.7% on placebo, and no serious event occurred in more than 3 patients in either arm. The most frequent were cardiac events and infections of the skin and soft tissue of the foot. There were 2 deaths in each arm, and the investigators judged that none was related to the study drug. The causes were myocardial infarction, sudden cardiac death, pneumonia and sepsis from a foot infection.

## 3.13 Adverse Events of Special Interest
- **Breathing:** Slow or shallow breathing was reported in 0.2% of the patients on NeuroVex and in no patient on placebo, and every patient with the event also took an opioid, a benzodiazepine or another drug that slows the brain. The patient with the event on NeuroVex was taking a stable low dose of an opioid, and no patient on placebo had the event.
- **Falls:** Fractures after a fall occurred in 4 patients on NeuroVex and 3 on placebo.
- **Hypersensitivity:** Angioedema was reported in 2 patients on NeuroVex and none on placebo, and both patients stopped treatment and recovered.
- **Vision:** Blurred vision, double vision and reduced visual acuity were reported in 3.3% of the patients on NeuroVex and 1.3% on placebo.
- **Mood:** Suicidal ideation was reported in 3 patients on NeuroVex and 2 on placebo. No suicide was completed.
- **Glucose:** Hypoglycemia was reported in 2.9% of the patients on NeuroVex and 2.7% on placebo, and the mean hemoglobin A1c did not change in a clinically relevant way in either arm.

## 3.14 Laboratory Values, Vital Signs and Electrocardiogram
Creatine kinase above 3 times the upper limit of normal was seen in 1.4% of the patients on NeuroVex and 0.8% on placebo, and most of the patients had no muscle symptoms. Alanine aminotransferase above 3 times the upper limit of normal occurred in 0.8% on NeuroVex and 0.6% on placebo. The mean serum creatinine rose by 0.04 mg/dL on NeuroVex and 0.02 mg/dL on placebo, and the difference was not progressive. The mean change in the systolic blood pressure was 0.8 mmHg on NeuroVex and 0.4 mmHg on placebo, and the mean weight gain was 0.9 kg on NeuroVex and 0.3 kg on placebo. The mean change in the QT interval corrected for the heart rate was less than 3 ms in both arms, and no patient had a corrected QT interval above 500 ms.

## 3.15 Plasma Levels of Neurovexin
In a subset of 212 patients on NeuroVex who gave blood samples, the mean trough plasma level of neurovexin at week 4 was 520 ng/mL in patients with a creatinine clearance of 60 mL/min or more and 1,010 ng/mL in patients with a creatinine clearance of 30 to 59 mL/min. The rate of dizziness was higher in patients with a creatinine clearance of 30 to 59 mL/min (18.7%) than in patients with a creatinine clearance of 60 mL/min or more (12.8%). The pain score drop was similar in the two groups.

## 3.17 Time Course of Dizziness
Dizziness began in the first 2 weeks in 71% of the patients on NeuroVex who had it and the median time to the first episode was 5 days. The median duration of the first episode was 9 days, and 52% of the episodes went away while the dose was unchanged. The proportion of patients with dizziness at each visit fell from 9.8% at week 2 to 4.1% at week 12 on NeuroVex, and from 2.6% to 1.7% on placebo. Dizziness was described as light-headedness by 63% of the patients, as a feeling that the room was spinning by 21% and as unsteadiness by 16%.

## 4.1 Conclusions
- In adults with painful diabetic peripheral neuropathy, NeuroVex 50mg twice daily lowered the daily pain score more than placebo at week 12: the drop was 2.4 points on NeuroVex and 1.1 points on placebo, a difference of 1.3 points (95% CI 0.9 to 1.7, p<0.001).
- More patients on NeuroVex than on placebo had at least 30% less pain (47% versus 29%) and at least 50% less pain (28% versus 15%), and pain interfered less with sleep (a drop of 2.1 points versus 0.9 points).
- The result was the same in the sensitivity analyses and in the subgroups, including patients 65 years or older and patients with a creatinine clearance of 30 to 59 mL/min.
- Dizziness and sleepiness were the main adverse reactions. Dizziness led to stopping in 8.2% of the patients on NeuroVex and 3.1% on placebo, and it was more common in patients 65 years or older (18.0% versus 11.4% under 65). Serious adverse events were similar in the two arms: 3.0% versus 2.7%.
- Slow or shallow breathing was seen only in patients who also took a drug that slows the brain.
- The benefit and the risk support the use of NeuroVex in the management of neuropathic pain from diabetic peripheral neuropathy in adults.

## 4.2 Limitations
The trial lasted 12 weeks, so it gives little information on the effect of longer treatment, which is described in an open-label extension without a control group. Patients with a creatinine clearance below 30 mL/min, patients younger than 18 years and patients with severe heart failure were excluded, and the results do not apply to them. All patients in the trial took 50mg twice daily, including those with a creatinine clearance of 30 to 59 mL/min, so the trial does not show the effect of a lower dose in patients with a reduced kidney function. The study did not compare NeuroVex with an active drug. The subgroup results are descriptive. The number of patients on a stable opioid was small, so the trial cannot give a firm estimate of the risk of slow breathing when NeuroVex is used with an opioid.

## 5.1 Protocol Amendments
The protocol had 2 amendments. The first, before the first patient was enrolled, added the key secondary endpoint on sleep. The second, after the first 400 patients were randomized, widened the allowed use of low-dose opioids from none to a stable dose of up to 30 mg of oral morphine equivalents a day, to make the trial population closer to patients in clinical practice. No change was made to the primary endpoint or to the statistical analysis after the database was locked.

## 5.2 Quality and Data Handling
All sites were monitored with a risk-based plan. The pain scores were recorded in an electronic diary, with a date and time stamp, and the compliance with the diary was 93% on NeuroVex and 94% on placebo. The study drug compliance, measured by the capsule count, was above 80% in 91% of the patients in each arm. Two sites were closed before the end of the trial for a failure to follow the protocol, and the data from the patients at these sites were included in the analyses up to the last valid visit. The conclusions did not change when these sites were left out.

## 5.3 Data Monitoring and Interim Review
The independent data monitoring committee met every 3 months and held a review of the safety data after 400 and after 800 patients had finished 12 weeks. At these reviews the committee looked at the serious adverse events, the deaths, the stops because of an adverse event and the events of breathing and of swelling. The committee did not ask for a stop or for a change in the protocol, and no interim analysis of the efficacy was done, so no adjustment of the significance level was needed.
