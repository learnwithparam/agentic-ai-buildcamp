# CARDIONEX (cardionexib) Package Insert, Japan

## 1 Warnings
**WARNING: Fetal toxicity.** Drugs that act directly on the renin-angiotensin system can cause injury and death to the developing fetus. CardioNex must not be given to pregnant women or women who may be pregnant [see 4.1 Contraindications]. When pregnancy is detected, discontinue CardioNex as soon as possible.

**Angioedema.** Angioedema with swelling of the face, lips, tongue or throat may occur and may be fatal when the airway is involved. If any such sign appears, discontinue CardioNex at once and do not restart it [see 6.1 Clinically significant adverse reactions].

Prescribers should be familiar with the treatment of chronic heart failure and should start CardioNex only at medical institutions where serum potassium and renal function can be measured.

## 2.1 Composition and description
Brand name: CardioNex Tablets 5 mg, CardioNex Tablets 10 mg, CardioNex Tablets 20 mg

Active ingredient: cardionexib. Each tablet contains 5 mg, 10 mg or 20 mg of cardionexib (as cardionexib hemisuccinate).

Excipients: lactose hydrate, crystalline cellulose, croscarmellose sodium, hydroxypropylcellulose, magnesium stearate, hypromellose, macrogol 4000, titanium oxide, and yellow ferric oxide (10 mg) or red ferric oxide (20 mg).

Description: film-coated tablets.

## 2.2 Dosage in Japan
- **Standard adult HFrEF (eGFR >= 30 mL/min):** Start at 10mg once daily. Raise to 20mg once daily after **8 weeks**.
- **Severe renal impairment (eGFR < 30 mL/min, no dialysis):** Give 5mg once daily. Do not raise the dose.

## 2.3 Administration in Japan
Take once daily after breakfast.

## 2.4 Precautions concerning dosage and administration
- The dose for patients with an eGFR of 30 mL/min or more and the dose for patients with severe renal impairment are given in 2.2 Dosage. Do not raise the dose in patients with severe renal impairment (eGFR below 30 mL/min, no dialysis).
- In patients with an eGFR of 30 mL/min or more, check serum potassium and serum creatinine before raising the dose from 10 mg to 20 mg after the 8 weeks stated in 2.2 Dosage. If a test result is abnormal, consider whether the dose should be raised.
- Patients who are taking a strong CYP3A4 inhibitor should not take more than 5 mg once daily [see 7.1 Interactions].
- Do not use CardioNex in patients on dialysis [see 4.1 Contraindications].
- A patient who has been taking an ACE inhibitor must not start CardioNex until 36 hours after the last ACE inhibitor dose [see 4.1 Contraindications].
- Tablets should be taken after breakfast, as stated in 2.3 Administration. If a dose is missed, the patient should take it later the same day and should not take two doses on one day.
- CardioNex Tablets 5 mg, 10 mg and 20 mg are not interchangeable in a mg-for-mg manner with any other agent that acts on the renin-angiotensin system. Doses must be set from 2.2 Dosage.

## 3.1 Approved use in Japan
Approved only for adults with chronic heart failure and an LVEF of 40% or lower.

## 3.2 Uses not approved in Japan
Not approved for heart failure with an LVEF above 40%, for high blood pressure alone, or for patients younger than 18 years.

## 3.3 Precautions concerning indications
- CardioNex should be used only in patients with chronic heart failure and an LVEF of 40% or lower. Efficacy in heart failure with an LVEF above 40% has not been shown [see 3.2 Uses not approved in Japan].
- Select patients after the clinical studies in 11 Clinical Studies have been fully understood. Patients with an eGFR below 30 mL/min, patients on dialysis, pregnant women and patients younger than 18 years were not enrolled in the global phase 3 study.
- CardioNex is not indicated for the treatment of high blood pressure alone, and it should not be used for that purpose.
- Use CardioNex together with the usual treatment of heart failure, and do not use it as a replacement for beta-blockers or for other treatment that is known to be effective, unless a physician judges that this is required.

## 4.1 Contraindications in Japan
- Patients taking an ACE inhibitor, or within **36 hours** of the last dose.
- Patients on dialysis.
- Pregnant women or women who may be pregnant.

## 4.2 Notes on the contraindications
- Patients taking an ACE inhibitor: Angioedema may occur. The 36 hours stated in 4.1 is counted from the time of the last ACE inhibitor dose to the time of the first CardioNex dose. When CardioNex is replaced by an ACE inhibitor, the labelling of the ACE inhibitor should also be followed.
- Patients on dialysis: Cardionexib is not removed by haemodialysis, and patients on dialysis were not studied.
- Pregnant women or women who may be pregnant: Fetal injury and death have been reported with drugs that act on the renin-angiotensin system. Women who may be pregnant should have a pregnancy test before the start of treatment where a physician finds this appropriate.
- Patients with a history of hypersensitivity to any ingredient of this drug: Do not use.

## 5.1 Careful administration in Japan
- Check serum potassium and creatinine before the first dose, at 2 weeks and at 8 weeks.
- Patients aged 75 or older: start at 5mg once daily.

## 5.2 Important precautions
- Hyperkalaemia may occur. Check serum potassium and serum creatinine before the first dose, at 2 weeks and at 8 weeks, as stated in 5.1, and at other times as clinically indicated, for example after a change in a diuretic or in a potassium-affecting drug.
- Symptomatic hypotension may occur. It is more likely in patients with volume depletion or on high-dose diuretics. Correct volume depletion before the first dose, and reduce the dose of the diuretic first if hypotension occurs.
- Renal function may worsen in the first weeks of treatment. In the global study, mean eGFR fell by 3 mL/min in the first month and did not fall further.
- Dizziness and hypotension may affect the ability to drive or operate machinery. Warn patients not to drive, work at heights or operate dangerous machinery until they know how this drug affects them.
- Patients should be told to contact a physician at once if the face, lips, tongue or throat swell, if they faint, or if the amount of urine falls markedly.
- Patients should be told not to stop treatment on their own, and not to use salt substitutes that contain potassium chloride without asking a physician.

## 5.3 Precautions for patients with renal impairment
- Patients with severe renal impairment (eGFR below 30 mL/min, no dialysis): Give 5 mg once daily as stated in 2.2 Dosage, and do not raise the dose. Cardionexib exposure rises as kidney function falls (AUC about 2-fold at an eGFR below 30 mL/min), and the risk of hyperkalaemia is higher. Check serum potassium and creatinine at the times stated in 5.1.
- Patients on dialysis: Contraindicated [see 4.1].
- Patients with an eGFR of 30 to 59 mL/min: AUC is about 32% higher than with normal function. No dose change is made, but follow the monitoring in 5.1.

## 5.4 Precautions for patients with hepatic impairment
- Patients with severe hepatic impairment (Child-Pugh C): CardioNex is not recommended. Exposure to cardionexib was about 2.2-fold higher, and no clinical data are available.
- Patients with mild or moderate hepatic impairment (Child-Pugh A or B): AUC was about 12% and 28% higher respectively. No dose change is needed, but watch the patient for dizziness and hypotension.

## 5.5 Precautions for women
- Pregnant women or women who may be pregnant: Contraindicated [see 4.1]. In animal studies in rats and rabbits, treatment in late gestation led to reduced fetal weight, delayed renal development and increased post-implantation loss.
- Women who could become pregnant should use effective contraception during treatment.
- Nursing women: Avoid treatment if possible. If treatment is needed, the patient should stop nursing. In rats, cardionexib is excreted in milk at levels close to those in plasma.

## 5.6 Precautions for children
The safety and efficacy of CardioNex in patients younger than 18 years have not been established (no clinical experience) [see 3.2].

## 5.7 Precautions for the elderly
In the global study, 38% of patients were 65 years or older and 14% were 75 years or older. No overall difference in efficacy was seen. Dizziness and hypotension were more frequent in older patients. Patients aged 75 or older start at 5 mg once daily as stated in 5.1, and the dose is then set according to tolerability. Cardionexib exposure was about 16% higher in patients aged 75 or older. Because renal and hepatic function often decline with age, check blood pressure at each visit during the first month and follow the monitoring in 5.1.

## 6.1 Clinically significant adverse reactions
1. Angioedema (less than 0.1%): Swelling of the face, lips, tongue or throat may occur. If any sign is seen, discontinue treatment at once and give appropriate treatment, including securing the airway. Never restart CardioNex.
2. Hyperkalaemia (5% or more): Check serum potassium regularly. Serum potassium above 5.5 mEq/L at any visit was seen in 4.1% of patients on CardioNex and in 1.9% on placebo in the global study, and in 7.6% of patients who also took spironolactone. If hyperkalaemia occurs, reduce the dose or discontinue treatment, and give appropriate treatment.
3. Symptomatic hypotension (5% or more): Low blood pressure, with dizziness or fainting, may occur. If it occurs, reduce the dose of the diuretic first, then consider reducing the dose or interrupting CardioNex.
4. Acute kidney injury (0.1% to less than 1%): Worsening of renal function may occur. Check renal function regularly, and if an abnormality is seen, discontinue treatment and give appropriate treatment.

## 6.3 Effects on laboratory tests
- Serum potassium: mean rise of 0.3 mEq/L by week 4, stable after that.
- Serum creatinine and eGFR: creatinine may rise in the first weeks, and mean eGFR fell by 3 mL/min in the first month.
- Blood urea nitrogen may rise slightly. Serum sodium below 130 mEq/L at any visit was seen in 1.4% of patients on CardioNex and 0.9% of patients on placebo.
- No clinically relevant effect was seen on liver function tests or on the QTc interval.

## 6.4 Post-marketing information
Frequencies in 6.1 and 6.2 come from the Japanese bridging study and the global study. The frequency of angioedema is less than 0.1%, and the frequency of acute kidney injury is 0.1% to less than 1%. The company collects safety data on 3,000 patients in Japan as stated in 9.1, and the label will be revised when those data are available.

## 7.1 Interactions in Japan
- **Strong CYP3A4 inhibitors:** 5mg once daily at most.
- **Spironolactone and eplerenone:** Check serum potassium every 2 weeks for the first 2 months.

## 7.2 Interaction table
Cardionexib is metabolised mainly by CYP3A4. It does not inhibit CYP3A4, CYP2C9, CYP2D6 or P-glycoprotein at clinically relevant concentrations.

| Drug | Sign, symptom and treatment | Mechanism and risk factor |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| ACE inhibitors (contraindicated, see 4.1) | Angioedema may occur. Give the first CardioNex dose only after 36 hours have passed since the last ACE inhibitor dose | Both act on the renin-angiotensin system |
| Strong CYP3A4 inhibitors (itraconazole, clarithromycin, ritonavir) | Exposure to cardionexib rises (about 3.5-fold with ketoconazole). Do not exceed 5 mg once daily | Inhibition of CYP3A4 metabolism |
| Strong CYP3A4 inducers (rifampicin, carbamazepine, phenytoin, St John's wort) | Exposure falls by about 70% and the effect may be lost. Avoid the combination | Induction of CYP3A4 |

Digoxin, warfarin, furosemide and atorvastatin changed in exposure by less than 15% when given with cardionexib, and no dose change is needed.

## 8.1 Overdosage
Signs and symptoms: Low blood pressure is the most likely effect. In healthy volunteers, single doses of up to 160 mg were tolerated.

Treatment: Supportive treatment, including fluids, with the patient supine. Check serum potassium and renal function. Cardionexib is highly protein bound (about 97%) and is not removed by haemodialysis.

## 8.2 Precautions concerning use
- Instruction at dispensing: Remove the tablet from the PTP sheet before taking it. A hard corner of the sheet may pierce the oesophageal mucosa and cause serious complications such as mediastinitis if the sheet is swallowed.
- The tablet is film-coated and should be swallowed with water. Patients should not crush the tablet.
- Storage: store at room temperature (1 to 30 degrees Celsius) in the original package, protected from moisture.

## 8.3 Other precautions
- In a 2-year study in rats and mice, no evidence of a carcinogenic effect was seen at exposures up to about 15 times the human exposure.
- Cardionexib was not genotoxic in a standard set of in vitro and in vivo tests.
- In juvenile rats dosed from postnatal day 7, delayed renal development and lower body weight were seen at higher exposures.

## 9.1 Post-marketing study in Japan
The company must collect safety data on 3,000 patients in Japan during the first 4 years after approval.

## 9.2 Other conditions of approval
- The company should make sure that the drug is used only by physicians with enough knowledge and experience in the treatment of chronic heart failure, at medical institutions that can respond to emergencies such as angioedema.
- The company should provide the physicians with information on the monitoring of serum potassium and renal function, and on the washout period after an ACE inhibitor, and should supply a leaflet for patients.
- The company should report the interim results of the post-marketing study in 9.1 to the Pharmaceuticals and Medical Devices Agency (PMDA) at the times it asks.

## 10.1 Blood concentrations
Healthy adult Japanese men took a single oral dose of CardioNex 20 mg. Peak plasma level (Cmax) was 405 ng/mL and was reached at about 2 hours (Tmax). The terminal half-life was about 14 hours and the AUC from time zero to infinity was 5,010 ng.h/mL.

| Parameter (20 mg once daily at steady state) | Japanese subjects (n=24) | Non-Japanese subjects (n=24) |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| Cmax, ng/mL | 428 | 412 |
| Tmax, h | 2.0 | 2.0 |
| AUC0-24, ng.h/mL | 5,070 | 4,860 |
| Half-life, h | 14.3 | 14.0 |

The pharmacokinetics are linear over 5 mg to 40 mg. Steady state is reached in 3 to 4 days, and the accumulation ratio is about 1.6. After correction for body weight, there was no clinically relevant difference between Japanese and non-Japanese subjects.

## 10.2 Absorption and food effect
Absolute bioavailability is about 71%. A high-fat meal lowered Cmax by about 11% and did not change AUC. In the Japanese clinical studies, the drug was taken after breakfast, as stated in 2.3.

## 10.3 Distribution
Plasma protein binding is about 97%, mainly to albumin. The apparent volume of distribution is 83 L.

## 10.4 Metabolism and excretion
Cardionexib is metabolised mainly in the liver by CYP3A4 to the metabolite M1, which has no relevant pharmacological activity. After a dose of radiolabelled cardionexib, about 52% of the radioactivity was recovered in faeces and about 41% in urine, nearly all as metabolites. Less than 5% of a dose is excreted unchanged in urine.

## 10.7 Pharmacokinetics in Japanese patients with heart failure
In the Japanese bridging study, steady-state trough concentrations of cardionexib at 20 mg once daily were stable between week 8 and week 52. Exposure in Japanese patients with heart failure was about 12% higher than in healthy Japanese adults, which is similar to the difference seen in non-Japanese patients. A population pharmacokinetic analysis found that eGFR was the covariate with the largest effect on clearance. Body weight, age, sex and race had small effects that were not clinically relevant. Co-administration of beta-blockers, mineralocorticoid receptor antagonists and loop diuretics did not change clearance.

## 11.1 Japanese bridging study (CLARION-J)
Purpose: To confirm that the efficacy and safety of CardioNex seen in the global study apply to Japanese patients, and to confirm the 8-week titration schedule in 2.2.

Design: A multicentre, randomised, double-blind, placebo-controlled phase 3 study at 38 sites in Japan. A total of 312 Japanese adults with chronic heart failure, an LVEF of 40% or lower and an eGFR of 30 mL/min or more were randomised 1 to 1 to CardioNex or placebo, added to the usual treatment. Patients on CardioNex started at 10 mg once daily after breakfast and the dose was raised to 20 mg once daily after 8 weeks. The treatment period was 52 weeks. Patients aged 75 or older started at 5 mg once daily.

Results: The primary outcome was the change in NT-proBNP from baseline to week 24. The ratio of the geometric mean in the CardioNex group to that in the placebo group was 0.82 (95% CI 0.73 to 0.92, p < 0.001).

| Outcome (week 52) | CardioNex (n=156) | Placebo (n=156) |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| Cardiovascular death or hospitalisation for heart failure, % | 11.5 | 15.4 |
| Hazard ratio (95% CI) | 0.73 (0.40 to 1.33) | |
| Mean change in eGFR at week 8, mL/min | -3.1 | -0.9 |
| Serum potassium above 5.5 mEq/L at any visit, % | 4.5 | 1.9 |
| Patients with an adverse reaction, % | 28.8 | 21.2 |
| Discontinuation due to an adverse reaction, % | 7.7 | 5.8 |

The study was not powered for clinical events, and the hazard ratio is consistent with the global result. The adverse reactions seen were hyperkalaemia, hypotension, dizziness and a rise in serum creatinine, and the pattern matched that of the global study.

## 11.2 Global phase 3 study (CLARION-HF)
A randomised, double-blind, placebo-controlled study in 4,820 adults with chronic heart failure, an LVEF of 40% or lower and an eGFR of 30 mL/min or more, with a median follow-up of 26 months. The primary outcome was the first of cardiovascular death or hospitalisation for heart failure. Japan took part in the study.

| Outcome | CardioNex | Placebo | Hazard ratio (95% CI) |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| Primary outcome | 14.2% | 19.1% | 0.74 (0.65 to 0.84) |
| Cardiovascular death | 7.1% | 8.9% | 0.79 (0.66 to 0.95) |

The doses used in the global study are not the doses approved in Japan. Use the doses in 2.2 Dosage.

## 11.4 Effect of the 8-week titration
In the Japanese bridging study, 91% of the patients on CardioNex who were eligible for the dose increase raised the dose from 10 mg to 20 mg once daily at week 8, and 86% were still taking 20 mg once daily at week 52. The reason for not raising the dose at week 8 was most often hypotension or a rise in serum potassium or creatinine.

## 11.5 Long-term treatment in Japan
In an open-label extension of the Japanese bridging study, 271 patients continued CardioNex for a further 52 weeks, giving a total exposure of up to 104 weeks. The doses were as set in 2.2 Dosage. Serum potassium and creatinine stayed stable after the first 8 weeks, and mean eGFR did not fall below the level seen at 8 weeks. The type and frequency of adverse reactions were the same as in the double-blind period, and no new safety signal was seen. Angioedema was not reported in the extension.

Blood pressure fell by a mean of 5 mmHg in systolic pressure from baseline at week 52 and stayed at that level in the extension. Patients who stopped treatment during the extension did so most often because of hypotension, a rise in serum creatinine or hyperkalaemia.

## 11.6 Efficacy by dose in the Japanese bridging study
At week 52, 86% of the Japanese patients on CardioNex were taking 20 mg once daily, and the NT-proBNP ratio against placebo at week 52 was 0.80 (95% CI 0.70 to 0.91). In patients who stayed on 10 mg once daily because of the results of the checks in 5.1, the ratio was 0.88. No patient group was excluded from the analysis on the basis of the dose actually taken, and the analysis followed the intention-to-treat principle.

## 12.1 Mechanism of action
Cardionexib is a single molecule, not a prodrug, that is both a selective, orally active antagonist of the angiotensin II type 1 (AT1) receptor and an inhibitor of neprilysin. Receptor blockade reduces vasoconstriction, sodium and water retention and aldosterone release driven by angiotensin II. Neprilysin inhibition slows the breakdown of natriuretic peptides and so raises their vasodilating and natriuretic effects. Together they lower the load on the failing heart. The reduced aldosterone effect leads to a rise in serum potassium. Cardionexib does not inhibit angiotensin-converting enzyme. Neprilysin and ACE both break down bradykinin, so bradykinin can build up when both are inhibited. This is the reason for the angioedema risk, for the contraindication with ACE inhibitors and for the 36-hour interval after the last ACE inhibitor dose stated in 4.1.

## 12.2 Pharmacological effects
- In patients with heart failure, CardioNex lowered mean systolic blood pressure by 4 to 7 mmHg and lowered NT-proBNP in a dose-dependent way.
- Plasma renin activity and angiotensin II rose, as expected with receptor blockade.
- Natriuretic peptide levels and urinary cGMP rose in a dose-dependent way through neprilysin inhibition, with a rise in urinary sodium excretion.
- Neprilysin also breaks down BNP, so BNP is not a reliable marker of heart failure during treatment and rises on CardioNex. NT-proBNP is not affected in this way and should be used instead.
- In a thorough QT study in healthy volunteers at doses up to 160 mg, there was no clinically relevant effect on heart rate or QTc.

## 12.3 Non-clinical pharmacology
- In rats with induced heart failure, cardionexib lowered left ventricular end-diastolic pressure, reduced cardiac fibrosis and improved survival compared with the vehicle.
- In enzyme assays, cardionexib inhibited neprilysin in the nanomolar range, with no inhibition of angiotensin-converting enzyme at concentrations above those reached in patients.
- In isolated tissue assays, cardionexib showed more than 10,000-fold selectivity for the angiotensin II type 1 receptor over the type 2 receptor.
- In dogs, cardionexib lowered blood pressure in a dose-dependent way, with a rise in plasma renin activity and in serum potassium.
- In safety pharmacology studies, no effect on respiratory function, central nervous system function or gastrointestinal motility was seen at exposures above those in humans.

## 12.4 Pharmacodynamics in Japanese subjects
In a study of healthy Japanese adults, serum potassium rose by a mean of 0.2 mEq/L after 7 days at 20 mg once daily, and the rise was larger in volunteers who also took spironolactone. Plasma angiotensin II rose in a dose-dependent way, as expected with receptor blockade. There was no difference in the pharmacodynamic response between Japanese and non-Japanese subjects after correction for body weight.
