# CARDIONEX (cardionexib) Summary of Product Characteristics, Germany

## 1 Name of the medicinal product
CardioNex 2.5 mg film-coated tablets
CardioNex 10 mg film-coated tablets
CardioNex 20 mg film-coated tablets

## 1.1 Approved use in Germany
CardioNex is approved in Germany to reduce cardiovascular death and hospitalization for heart failure in adults with chronic heart failure and an LVEF of 40% or lower.

## 2.1 Qualitative and quantitative composition
Each film-coated tablet contains 2.5 mg, 10 mg or 20 mg of cardionexib (as cardionexib hemisuccinate).

For the full list of excipients, see section 6.2.

## 2.2 Dosage in Germany
- **Standard adult HFrEF (eGFR >= 30 mL/min):** Start at 10mg once daily. Raise to 20mg once daily after 4 weeks.
- **Severe renal impairment (eGFR < 30 mL/min, no dialysis):** Give 2.5mg once daily. Do not raise the dose.

## 2.3 Administration in Germany
Take once daily at about the same time, with or without food. The tablet may be split along its score line.

## 3 Pharmaceutical form
Film-coated tablet.

CardioNex 2.5 mg: white, round, biconvex film-coated tablet of 6.0 mm diameter with a score line on both sides and the engraving "CX" on one side.

CardioNex 10 mg: pale yellow, oval, biconvex film-coated tablet of 10.5 mm length with a score line on both sides and the engraving "CX 10" on one side.

CardioNex 20 mg: pink, oval, biconvex film-coated tablet of 13.0 mm length with a score line on both sides and the engraving "CX 20" on one side.

The score line is not intended for dividing the tablet into equal doses. It is there to make the tablet easier to split for swallowing, as stated in section 2.3.

## 4.1 Therapeutic indications
CardioNex is indicated in adults for the reduction of cardiovascular death and hospitalisation for heart failure in chronic heart failure with an LVEF of 40% or lower (heart failure with reduced ejection fraction, HFrEF). The indication is stated in section 1.1.

CardioNex has not been studied in heart failure with an LVEF above 40%, and no efficacy claim is made for that group. The safety and efficacy of CardioNex in children and adolescents below 18 years of age have not been established [see section 4.2].

## 4.2 Posology and method of administration
Treatment with CardioNex should be started and supervised by a physician experienced in the treatment of heart failure.

Posology

The recommended doses for adults are given in section 2.2. In short, patients with an eGFR of 30 mL/min or more start with 10 mg once daily and the dose is raised to 20 mg once daily after 4 weeks. Patients with severe renal impairment (eGFR below 30 mL/min, not on dialysis) receive 2.5 mg once daily and the dose is not raised. Section 2.2 is the governing statement of the dose in Germany, and nothing in this section changes it.

Before the first dose, the prescriber should confirm that the patient has not taken an ACE inhibitor within the last 48 hours [see section 4.3], that serum potassium is not above 5.0 mmol/L [see section 4.4], and that the patient is not on dialysis [see section 4.3].

Missed dose

If a dose is missed, it should be taken on the same day as soon as the patient remembers. The patient should not take two doses on the same day to make up for a forgotten dose.

Special populations

Elderly patients: No dose rule specific to age is set for Germany beyond the doses in section 2.2. Because older patients had more dizziness and low blood pressure in the clinical programme, blood pressure should be checked at each visit in the first month of treatment. Of the patients in the main trial, 38% were 65 years or older and 14% were 75 years or older.

Renal impairment: The dose in patients with an eGFR below 30 mL/min who are not on dialysis is given in section 2.2. CardioNex is contraindicated in patients on dialysis [see section 4.3]. Patients with an eGFR below 30 mL/min were not enrolled in the main trial [see section 5.1].

Hepatic impairment: No dose change is needed in mild or moderate hepatic impairment (Child-Pugh A or B). CardioNex is not recommended in severe hepatic impairment (Child-Pugh C) [see section 4.4].

Paediatric population: The safety and efficacy of CardioNex in children and adolescents below 18 years of age have not been established. No data are available.

Method of administration

CardioNex is for oral use. The administration advice is in section 2.3: one tablet once daily, at about the same time of day, with or without food. The tablet may be split along its score line, and it should be swallowed with a glass of water without chewing the fragments.

The patient should be told not to take both medicines, and the pharmacy should be asked to remove the ACE inhibitor from the repeat prescription.

## 4.3 Contraindications in Germany
- Do not combine with ACE inhibitors. Wait **48 hours** after the last ACE inhibitor dose before the first CardioNex dose.
- Do not use in patients on dialysis.

## 4.3.1 Notes on the contraindications
ACE inhibitors: The contraindication is based on the risk of angioedema. Neprilysin and ACE both break down bradykinin, so bradykinin can build up when both are inhibited within a short time. The interval of 48 hours after the last ACE inhibitor dose, stated in section 4.3, is the interval for Germany. A prescriber who finds a different interval in the labelling of another country should follow the German labelling for patients treated in Germany.

Dialysis: Patients on dialysis were not studied, and cardionexib is not removed by haemodialysis, so a dose cannot be corrected by timing it around a session. The contraindication in section 4.3 therefore applies to all dialysis patients, whatever their residual kidney function.

Hypersensitivity: CardioNex must not be given to patients who are hypersensitive to cardionexib or to any excipient listed in section 6.2.

## 4.4 Special warnings in Germany
- **Potassium:** Check serum potassium before the first dose and 1 week after each dose change. Do not start if serum potassium is above 5.0 mmol/L.
- **Angioedema:** Stop at once if the face, lips, tongue or throat swell, and never restart.
- **Severe hepatic impairment (Child-Pugh C):** Not recommended.

## 4.4.1 Further information on potassium monitoring
Cardionexib blocks the angiotensin II type 1 receptor, which lowers aldosterone-driven potassium excretion in the kidney tubule, so serum potassium can rise [see section 5.1]. In clinical practice the effect is largest in patients with an eGFR below 60 mL/min, in patients with diabetes, and in patients who also take a potassium-sparing diuretic or a potassium supplement.

The monitoring schedule in section 4.4 applies to all patients: serum potassium is measured before the first dose and 1 week after each dose change, and treatment is not started if serum potassium is above 5.0 mmol/L. Patients who also take a potassium-sparing diuretic or a potassium supplement are monitored as described in section 4.5.

In CLARION-HF, mean serum potassium rose by 0.3 mmol/L by week 4 and then stayed level. Serum potassium above 5.5 mmol/L at any visit was seen in 4.1% of patients on CardioNex and 1.9% on placebo. Among patients who also took spironolactone, serum potassium above 5.5 mmol/L was seen in 7.6%. Patients with a screening serum potassium above 5.2 mmol/L were excluded from the trial.

Prescribers should be aware that a haemolysed blood sample can give a falsely high potassium result. If a result is unexpectedly high in a patient without symptoms, the sample may be repeated before treatment is changed. Patients should be told to report muscle weakness, palpitations or an irregular heartbeat, which can be signs of high potassium, and to avoid salt substitutes that contain potassium chloride.

## 4.4.2 Further information on angioedema
Angioedema has been reported rarely in patients treated with cardionexib. If the face, lips, tongue or throat swell, CardioNex must be stopped at once, the airway must be secured, and the patient must receive appropriate treatment and be observed until the swelling has fully gone. CardioNex must never be restarted, as stated in section 4.4.

Swelling that is limited to the face and lips usually resolves without treatment, and antihistamines can help to relieve symptoms. Angioedema involving the tongue, glottis or larynx may be fatal because of airway obstruction. Emergency treatment, including subcutaneous adrenaline and measures to keep the airway open, should be given without delay.

Patients with a history of angioedema of any cause were not studied in the clinical programme. Patients who have had angioedema while taking an ACE inhibitor should be considered to be at risk, and the prescriber should weigh the benefit against that risk before any use. The combination with ACE inhibitors is contraindicated, and the 48-hour interval in section 4.3 must be kept [see section 4.5.1].

## 4.4.3 Further information on hypotension and renal function
Symptomatic hypotension is most likely in patients who are volume depleted, for example by high-dose diuretic treatment, reduced salt intake, diarrhoea or vomiting. Volume depletion should be corrected before the first dose. If hypotension occurs, the dose of a concomitant diuretic should be lowered first. A temporary interruption of CardioNex may then be considered. Hypotension is not in itself a reason to stop treatment permanently, and treatment can usually be continued once blood pressure has stabilised.

Serum creatinine may rise in the first weeks of treatment. In CLARION-HF, mean eGFR fell by 3 mL/min in the first month and did not fall further. Renal function should be assessed at baseline and as clinically indicated afterwards. A marked rise in creatinine in the first weeks should lead the prescriber to review the diuretic dose and other medicinal products that affect the kidney, for example non-steroidal anti-inflammatory drugs.

In patients with bilateral renal artery stenosis or stenosis of the artery to a single functioning kidney, medicinal products that act on the renin-angiotensin system may increase blood urea and serum creatinine. No data are available for cardionexib in these patients.

## 4.4.4 Hepatic impairment and other populations
CardioNex is not recommended in severe hepatic impairment (Child-Pugh C), because exposure to cardionexib is more than doubled and no clinical data are available [see section 5.2]. In mild or moderate hepatic impairment (Child-Pugh A or B), no dose change is needed.

Patients on dialysis, patients with an eGFR below 30 mL/min, patients with an LVEF above 40%, pregnant women and patients younger than 18 years were not enrolled in CLARION-HF [see section 5.1]. CardioNex is contraindicated in patients on dialysis [see section 4.3].

## 4.4.5 Excipients
CardioNex contains lactose monohydrate. Patients with rare hereditary problems of galactose intolerance, total lactase deficiency or glucose-galactose malabsorption should not take this medicinal product.

CardioNex contains less than 1 mmol sodium (23 mg) per tablet, that is to say it is essentially "sodium-free".

## 4.4.6 Information for patients
Patients should be told to take CardioNex once daily at about the same time each day, to keep a note of their blood pressure if they have a home monitor, and to attend every blood test that is booked after the start of treatment or a dose change. Patients should be asked to report the following without delay:

- swelling of the face, lips, tongue or throat, or trouble breathing or swallowing, which are signs of angioedema, in which case treatment must be stopped and must not be restarted;
- fainting, severe dizziness, or a marked fall in the amount of urine;
- muscle weakness, tingling or an irregular heartbeat;
- pregnancy, or a plan to become pregnant;
- any new medicinal product, including potassium supplements, salt substitutes, anti-inflammatory painkillers and herbal products that contain St John's wort.

Patients should be reminded that CardioNex is not to be taken together with an ACE inhibitor, and that they must say that they take CardioNex whenever a doctor or pharmacist offers them a new medicine.

## 4.4.7 Use in patients with heart failure and other conditions
Patients with an LVEF above 40%: The efficacy of CardioNex in heart failure with an LVEF above 40% has not been shown, and the indication in section 1.1 is limited to an LVEF of 40% or lower.

Patients after recent myocardial infarction, patients with symptomatic hypotension at rest, and patients with a systolic blood pressure below 100 mmHg before the start were few in CLARION-HF, and the experience in these groups is limited. Treatment should be started with particular care in them, and blood pressure should be checked more often.

Surgery and anaesthesia: Hypotension may occur during anaesthesia in patients who take medicinal products that act on the renin-angiotensin system. The anaesthetist should be told that the patient takes CardioNex.

## 4.5 Interactions in Germany
- **Strong CYP3A4 inhibitors** (such as itraconazole or clarithromycin): give no more than 2.5mg once daily.
- **Potassium-sparing diuretics and potassium supplements:** Check serum potassium weekly for the first month.

## 4.5.1 Combination with ACE inhibitors and other renin-angiotensin agents
The combination of CardioNex with an ACE inhibitor is contraindicated because of the risk of angioedema [see section 4.3]. The first CardioNex dose must not be given before 48 hours have passed since the last ACE inhibitor dose, as stated in section 4.3. When a patient is changed from CardioNex to an ACE inhibitor, no CardioNex dose should be given on the day the ACE inhibitor is started, and the prescriber should check the labelling of the ACE inhibitor for its own washout advice.

Combination with an angiotensin receptor blocker or with aliskiren is not recommended. Dual blockade of the renin-angiotensin system increases the risk of hypotension, hyperkalaemia and a fall in renal function, and no benefit was shown in the clinical programme.

## 4.5.2 Pharmacokinetic interactions
Effect of other medicinal products on cardionexib: Cardionexib is cleared mainly by metabolism through CYP3A4 [see section 5.2]. Strong CYP3A4 inhibitors such as itraconazole or clarithromycin raise exposure to cardionexib, and no more than 2.5 mg once daily should be given, as stated in section 4.5. In an interaction study, ketoconazole 400 mg once daily raised the AUC of cardionexib about 3.5-fold and the Cmax about 1.9-fold. Moderate CYP3A4 inhibitors such as erythromycin or diltiazem raised the AUC about 1.6-fold, which is not considered clinically relevant, but blood pressure should be watched when treatment with a moderate inhibitor is started.

Strong CYP3A4 inducers such as rifampicin, carbamazepine, phenytoin and St John's wort lower exposure to cardionexib by about 70% and may remove its benefit. The combination should be avoided.

Effect of cardionexib on other medicinal products: Cardionexib is not an inhibitor of CYP3A4, CYP2C9, CYP2D6 or P-glycoprotein at clinically relevant concentrations. In interaction studies it did not change the exposure to digoxin, warfarin, furosemide or atorvastatin to a clinically relevant extent.

## 4.5.3 Pharmacodynamic interactions
Antihypertensive medicinal products, diuretics, nitrates and alcohol may add to the blood-pressure-lowering effect of CardioNex. Non-steroidal anti-inflammatory drugs, including selective cyclo-oxygenase-2 inhibitors, may reduce the effect of CardioNex and may worsen renal function and raise serum potassium, particularly in elderly or volume-depleted patients. Renal function and serum potassium should be monitored when they are used together.

Trimethoprim, heparin and ciclosporin can also raise serum potassium. Potassium-sparing diuretics and potassium supplements are covered in section 4.5, where the monitoring for them is stated. Lithium: reversible rises in serum lithium and lithium toxicity have been reported with medicinal products that act on the renin-angiotensin system, so serum lithium should be monitored if the combination is needed.

## 4.6 Pregnancy and breastfeeding in Germany
Do not use in the second or third trimester. Stop as soon as pregnancy is found. Do not breastfeed during treatment.

## 4.6.1 Women of childbearing potential and pregnancy
Women of childbearing potential should use effective contraception during treatment with CardioNex. Medicinal products that act directly on the renin-angiotensin system can cause fetal injury and death when given in the second and third trimester. CardioNex must not be used in the second or third trimester, and treatment must be stopped as soon as pregnancy is found, as stated in section 4.6. A treatment that is safe in pregnancy should then be chosen.

There are limited data from the use of cardionexib in pregnant women. Studies in animals have shown reproductive toxicity [see section 5.3]. Infants whose mothers took a renin-angiotensin agent in late pregnancy should be checked for hypotension, oliguria and hyperkalaemia.

## 4.6.2 Breast-feeding and fertility
It is not known whether cardionexib passes into human breast milk. In animal studies it was found in milk at levels close to those in plasma. Breastfeeding must not take place during treatment, as stated in section 4.6.

Fertility: No effect on fertility was seen in male or female rats at exposures up to about 12 times the human exposure at the 20 mg dose. There are no human fertility data.

## 4.7 Effects on ability to drive and use machines
CardioNex has a minor to moderate influence on the ability to drive and use machines. Dizziness and low blood pressure are common, and they are most likely in the first weeks of treatment and after a dose change. Patients should be told not to drive or operate machinery until they know how CardioNex affects them, and to sit or lie down if they feel faint.

## 4.8 Side effects in Germany
Very common (more than 1 in 10): none. Common (up to 1 in 10): low blood pressure, high potassium, dizziness and a rise in creatinine. Rare (up to 1 in 1,000): angioedema.

## 4.8.1 Summary of the safety profile
The safety of CardioNex was assessed in CLARION-HF, a placebo-controlled phase 3 trial in 4,820 adults with chronic heart failure and an LVEF of 40% or lower, with a median follow-up of 26 months. In this trial, 7.9% of patients on CardioNex and 6.1% of patients on placebo stopped treatment because of an adverse reaction.

The most frequent adverse reactions were low blood pressure (symptomatic hypotension in 9.1% of patients on CardioNex against 4.5% on placebo), high potassium (6.8% against 3.4%), dizziness (6.3% against 3.9%) and a rise in creatinine (worsening renal function in 4.2% against 3.8%). Angioedema is rare and is the most serious adverse reaction [see section 4.4].

## 4.8.3 Description of selected adverse reactions
Hyperkalaemia: Serum potassium above 5.5 mmol/L at any visit was seen in 4.1% of patients on CardioNex and 1.9% on placebo. The mean rise at week 4 was 0.3 mmol/L. The rate was 7.6% in patients who also took spironolactone. Most episodes were mild and were managed by monitoring and, where needed, by a change in the concomitant medicinal product. See sections 4.4, 4.4.1 and 4.5.

Hypotension and dizziness: Low blood pressure with dizziness or fainting was most frequent in volume-depleted patients and in patients on high-dose diuretics, and it was seen most often in the first weeks. Older patients had more dizziness and hypotension. See section 4.4.3.

Renal function: Serum creatinine rose in the first weeks, and mean eGFR fell by 3 mL/min in the first month and did not fall further. Rises in creatinine were reversible in most patients after review of the diuretic dose.

Angioedema: Cases of angioedema involving the face, lips, tongue or throat were reported rarely. See sections 4.4 and 4.4.2.

## 4.8.4 Paediatric population and reporting of suspected adverse reactions
CardioNex has not been studied in patients younger than 18 years, and no paediatric safety data are available.

Reporting suspected adverse reactions after authorisation of the medicinal product is important. It allows continued monitoring of the benefit-risk balance of the medicinal product. ## 4.9 Overdose
Experience of overdose in humans is limited. In healthy volunteers, single doses of up to 160 mg were tolerated, with transient dizziness and low blood pressure at the higher doses.

The most likely effect of an overdose is low blood pressure. If symptomatic hypotension occurs, the patient should be placed supine, and supportive treatment, including intravenous fluids, should be given. Serum potassium and renal function should be checked, because hyperkalaemia and a fall in renal function may follow. Cardionexib is highly protein bound (about 97%) and is not removed by haemodialysis.

## 5.1 Pharmacodynamic properties
Pharmacotherapeutic group: agents acting on the renin-angiotensin system; angiotensin II receptor blockers, other combinations. ATC code: C09DX99.

Mechanism of action

Cardionexib is a single molecule, not a prodrug, that is both a selective, orally active antagonist of the angiotensin II type 1 (AT1) receptor and an inhibitor of neprilysin. By blocking the AT1 receptor, it reduces vasoconstriction, sodium and water retention and aldosterone release driven by angiotensin II. By inhibiting neprilysin, it slows the breakdown of natriuretic peptides, which raises their levels and their effects (vasodilation, natriuresis and diuresis) and lowers the load on the failing heart. The reduced aldosterone effect also explains the rise in serum potassium seen with treatment [see section 4.4.1]. Cardionexib does not inhibit angiotensin-converting enzyme. Neprilysin and ACE both break down bradykinin, so inhibiting neprilysin while ACE is also inhibited lets bradykinin build up, which is the mechanism behind angioedema. This is why the combination with ACE inhibitors, and any dose within 48 hours of the last ACE inhibitor dose, is contraindicated [see section 4.3].

Pharmacodynamic effects

In patients with heart failure, single and repeated doses lowered mean systolic blood pressure by 4 to 7 mmHg, with the largest effect 2 to 4 hours after the dose, and lowered NT-proBNP in a dose-dependent way. Plasma renin activity and plasma angiotensin II rose, as expected with receptor blockade. Through neprilysin inhibition, natriuretic peptide levels rose and urinary cyclic guanosine monophosphate (cGMP) rose in a dose-dependent way, with a rise in urinary sodium excretion. Because neprilysin also breaks down BNP, BNP is not a reliable marker of heart failure in patients on CardioNex and rises on treatment, whereas NT-proBNP is not a neprilysin substrate and can be used to follow the patient. There was no clinically relevant effect on heart rate or on the QTc interval at doses up to 160 mg in a thorough QT study.

Clinical efficacy and safety

CLARION-HF was a randomised, double-blind, placebo-controlled, event-driven phase 3 trial. A total of 4,820 adults with chronic heart failure, an LVEF of 40% or lower and an eGFR of 30 mL/min or more were randomised 1 to 1 to CardioNex or placebo, on top of usual care. The median follow-up was 26 months. The primary outcome was the first occurrence of cardiovascular death or hospitalisation for heart failure.

The primary outcome occurred in 14.2% of patients on CardioNex and 19.1% on placebo (hazard ratio 0.74, 95% CI 0.65 to 0.84, p < 0.001).

Paediatric population

The European Medicines Agency has waived the obligation to submit the results of studies with CardioNex in all subsets of the paediatric population in heart failure with reduced ejection fraction, because a study in children is not considered feasible.

All patients were randomised to double-blind treatment and were followed for the primary outcome until the required number of events had been reached. Cardiovascular death and hospitalisation for heart failure were assessed by an independent clinical events committee that did not know the treatment allocation. The analysis of the primary outcome used a Cox model stratified by region and by baseline eGFR group, on the intention-to-treat population.

The benefit seen in the primary outcome was consistent across the pre-specified subgroups of age, sex, diabetes status, baseline eGFR group and use of a mineralocorticoid receptor antagonist.

## 5.1.2 Further efficacy findings
Over the follow-up period, the event curves for the primary outcome separated within the first 3 months and continued to diverge. The number needed to treat to prevent one primary outcome event over 26 months was about 20.

Symptoms and function: On the Kansas City Cardiomyopathy Questionnaire clinical summary score, patients on CardioNex had a mean improvement over placebo of 2.1 points at 12 months (95% CI 1.2 to 3.0). The share of patients whose NYHA class improved by at least one class at 12 months was 21% on CardioNex and 17% on placebo.

Biomarkers: Median NT-proBNP fell by 14% from baseline at 8 months on CardioNex and rose by 3% on placebo.

Safety in the older population: In patients aged 75 years or older, dizziness and low blood pressure were more frequent on CardioNex than on placebo, in line with the pattern for all patients. The benefit on the primary outcome was consistent with that of the whole population.

Use with a mineralocorticoid receptor antagonist: In patients who took spironolactone or eplerenone at baseline, the benefit on the primary outcome was consistent with the overall result. Serum potassium above 5.5 mmol/L at any visit was seen in 7.6% of the patients who took spironolactone, as described in section 4.4.1.

## 5.1.3 Pharmacodynamic studies in patients and volunteers
Blood pressure: In a 12-week study in patients with heart failure, CardioNex lowered mean seated systolic blood pressure by 5 mmHg (95% CI 3 to 7 mmHg) from baseline at the 20 mg dose, with a smaller fall at 10 mg. The effect on blood pressure did not increase after week 4.

Renal haemodynamics: In a study of 64 patients with an eGFR of 30 to 59 mL/min, measured glomerular filtration rate fell by 2 to 4 mL/min within 2 weeks of the start and then stayed stable. The fall is expected with a drug that acts on the renin-angiotensin system and is consistent with the eGFR findings in CLARION-HF.

Cardiac repolarisation: In a thorough QT study in 56 healthy volunteers, single doses of 20 mg and 160 mg did not prolong the QTc interval. The upper bound of the 95% confidence interval for the largest placebo-corrected mean change from baseline in QTcF was below 10 ms at both doses, and the positive control showed assay sensitivity.

Potassium: In healthy volunteers, serum potassium rose by a mean of 0.2 mmol/L after 7 days at 20 mg. The rise was larger in volunteers who also took a potassium-sparing diuretic.

## 5.2 Pharmacokinetic properties
The pharmacokinetics of cardionexib are linear over the dose range of 2.5 mg to 40 mg.

| Parameter (20 mg once daily, steady state, healthy adults) | Mean value |
| :--- | :--- |
| Time to peak plasma level (Tmax) | about 2 hours (range 1 to 3.5 hours) |
| Cmax | 412 ng/mL |
| AUC over 24 hours (AUC0-24) | 4,860 ng.h/mL |
| Absolute bioavailability | about 71% |
| Apparent oral clearance (CL/F) | 4.1 L/h |
| Apparent volume of distribution (Vz/F) | 83 L |
| Terminal half-life | about 14 hours |
| Time to steady state | 3 to 4 days |
| Accumulation ratio | about 1.6 |
| Protein binding | about 97% |
| Fraction excreted unchanged in urine | less than 5% |

Absorption: Cardionexib is rapidly absorbed after oral dosing, and peak plasma levels are reached about 2 hours after a dose. A high-fat meal lowered Cmax by about 11% and did not change AUC, so CardioNex can be taken with or without food.

Distribution: Cardionexib is highly bound to plasma proteins (about 97%), mainly albumin. The apparent volume of distribution is 83 L. In rats, cardionexib was found in milk at levels close to those in plasma.

Biotransformation: Cardionexib is cleared mainly by metabolism in the liver. The main pathway is oxidation by CYP3A4 to the metabolite M1, which has no relevant pharmacological activity and makes up about 18% of drug-related material in plasma.

Elimination: After a single oral dose of radiolabelled cardionexib, about 52% of the radioactivity was recovered in faeces and about 41% in urine, nearly all as metabolites. Less than 5% of a dose is excreted unchanged in urine. The terminal half-life is about 14 hours, and steady state is reached in 3 to 4 days.

## 5.2.2 Population pharmacokinetic analysis
A population pharmacokinetic model was built on pooled data from 3,918 patients and healthy volunteers. Renal function, expressed as eGFR, was the covariate with the largest effect on apparent clearance. Clearance fell by about 1.1% for each 1 mL/min fall in eGFR below 60 mL/min. Age, body weight, sex and race had small effects that were not clinically relevant. Concomitant use of beta-blockers, mineralocorticoid receptor antagonists and loop diuretics did not affect clearance.

## 5.2.3 Pharmacokinetics in patients with heart failure
In patients with chronic heart failure, steady-state exposure to cardionexib at 20 mg once daily was about 12% higher than in healthy adults, and the half-life was unchanged at about 14 hours. Trough concentrations at steady state were stable between weeks 4 and 26, which shows no loss of exposure over time. The peak-to-trough ratio at steady state was about 4.6, which supports once-daily dosing.

Intake with food: The effect of a high-fat meal on Cmax was small and did not change AUC, so the advice in section 2.3 to take the tablet with or without food applies to patients with heart failure as it does to healthy adults.

Splitting the tablet: A study of 24 healthy adults showed that a tablet split along its score line gave the same AUC as a whole tablet, with a Cmax within 8% of the whole tablet.

## 5.3 Preclinical safety data
Non-clinical data reveal no special hazard for humans based on conventional studies of safety pharmacology, repeated dose toxicity, genotoxicity and carcinogenic potential.

In repeated dose toxicity studies in rats and dogs, the main findings were the expected pharmacological effects of a drug that acts on the renin-angiotensin system: lower blood pressure, reduced kidney weight with juxtaglomerular cell hypertrophy, and small rises in serum potassium and blood urea. These findings were reversible after the end of dosing.

Reproductive toxicity: Fertility in male and female rats was not affected at exposures up to about 12 times the human exposure at 20 mg once daily. In pregnant rats and rabbits, treatment in late gestation led to reduced fetal weight, delayed renal development and increased post-implantation loss at exposures at or above those in humans. These findings are in line with the class effect of medicinal products that act on the renin-angiotensin system and support the advice in sections 4.3 and 4.6 to stop treatment as soon as pregnancy is found.

Carcinogenicity: Two-year studies in rats and mice gave no evidence of a carcinogenic effect at exposures up to about 15 times the human exposure.

Environmental risk assessment: Cardionexib is not expected to pose a risk to the aquatic environment at the expected use.

## 5.3.1 Repeated dose and juvenile toxicology
In a 26-week study in rats and a 52-week study in dogs, the no-observed-adverse-effect level was at least 6 times the human exposure at 20 mg once daily. The kidney changes at higher doses were reversible, with no tubular necrosis, and the rises in blood urea were small and reversible.

In juvenile rats dosed from postnatal day 7, higher exposures were linked with delayed renal development and lower body weight, which is a known effect of the renin-angiotensin class in young animals. This supports the advice that CardioNex should not be used in children below 18 years of age.
